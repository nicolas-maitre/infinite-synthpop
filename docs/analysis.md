# Musical analysis: "True Faith" × "The Different Story" × "You Came"

This is the research the generator is built on. It covers New Order's **"True Faith"** (1987) and

share, where they differ, and how each finding becomes a rule in the code. A third reference,
Kim Wilde's **"You Came"** (1988), is measured in §6: the major-key corner of the synthpop genre,
next to the two near-identical minor-key songs. §7 says what it adds to the generator.

There are two layers of evidence:

1. **Measured from the recordings** (§1–§3, §6): signal analysis of the five files in
   `inspiration/` ("True Faith", the "Different Story" single version and its 2023 long version,
   and "You Came" in its album version and 12″ extended version). The script is
   [`scripts/analyze_reference.py`](../scripts/analyze_reference.py), and its raw output is
   [`reference-data.json`](reference-data.json). Parts are transcribed from Demucs stems with
   [`scripts/transcribe_bass.py`](../scripts/transcribe_bass.py) and
   [`scripts/transcribe_drums.py`](../scripts/transcribe_drums.py). **Where the two layers
   disagree, the generator follows the measurements.**
2. **Written sources** (§4): Hooktheory, Sound On Sound, Wikipedia, a fan archive and tab sites.
   These cover the production facts that audio analysis can't recover (which machines, who played
   what).

How reliable the measurements are: chords are estimated by matching triad templates against
harmonic-only chroma at half-bar resolution. Passing chords and bars dominated by vocals or riffs
can be misread (for example, the stray "Cm" readings in "True Faith" are most likely C plus an E♭
from the lead lines). The form comes from automatic segmentation plus my reading of the chord
sequences. Timestamps are accurate to about a bar.

### Shared DNA

1. **Nearly the same tempo (117 and 119.8 BPM) and the same minor engine.** The verse climbs **i → VI → VII → v** in both
   songs, the "epic minor" climb through the two major chords ♭VI and ♭VII. Both verses sit on
   the **minor v**, the melancholy, unresolved dominant.
2. **The same chorus shape, i … VII → IV/iv.** It opens on the tonic, swings through ♭VII and
   lands on the subdominant. The two choruses differ by **a single note**:
   - "True Faith" uses the **major IV** (G–B♮–D, Dorian).
   - "The Different Story" uses the **minor iv** (A–C–E, Aeolian).
3. **Dorian colour in both.** "True Faith" uses the major IV in its chorus, its bridge vamp and
   its whole outro. "The Different Story" uses the Dorian **ii → IV** (F♯m → A) in its
   turnaround. Both brighten the minor key by raising the 6th degree.
4. **The same form logic.** An instrumental intro over the chorus chords, 16-bar verses, 8-bar
   choruses, instrumental sections built from existing progressions, and a final run of repeated
   choruses.
5. **Machine-steady drums.** Four-on-the-floor kick and busy 16th-note hats and sequences.

### Where they differ

- **Ending the verse.** DS turns its second verse line toward the **harmonic-minor major V**
  (B major, with the D♯ leading tone). That gives the continental "Euro" pull back to the tonic.
  TF stays modal the whole time.
- **The outro.** TF's chorus chord **IV (G) becomes the new tonic**: the song modulates up a
  fourth, from minor to major, and vamps there for 32 bars. DS fades on the chorus.
- **The bass** (see the figure below): the same figure in both, from the first bars to the end
  (TF's from 0:08). Only the approach notes differ slightly. Peter Hook's melodic bass guitar
  plays over the top in TF's intro, bridge and outro. From after its first chorus, TF adds a high
  16th sequencer pedal on the key's 5th.

## 4. From the written sources

- **True Faith** was produced by Stephen Hague with New Order, at Advision Studio One.
  - **Drums:** programmed on an **E-mu SP-12**. Stephen Morris overdubbed real hi-hat and cymbals.
  - **Bass:** most bass sounds came from a **DX5**, and the sequenced bass idea came from a
    New York dance track. Peter Hook played the melodic high-register bass through an EHX Clone
    Theory **chorus** pedal, on the intro and outro themes and a bass solo.
  - **Pad:** the harmonic backbone was an **Emulator II + Octave Voyetra-8** pad. Sumner played
    DI'd, overdriven guitar.
  - **Chorus vocals:** two voices sing the chorus in the high octave. (Sources: Sound On Sound,
    Wikipedia.)
- **The Different Story** is Schilling's 1989 English-only single, made in collaboration with
  Michael Cretu (Enigma). It was a top-10 hit in Sweden and reached #16 on the Billboard Dance
  chart.
- **You Came** was written by Ricky and Kim Wilde and produced by **Ricky Wilde and Tony Swain**
  (of Swain & Jolley, the Bananarama and Imagination producers) for _Close_ (MCA, 1988), recorded
  1987–88 at the family's **Select Sound** studio in Knebworth. It was the album's second single,
  released 4 July 1988: UK #3, #2 on the Eurochart, #1 in Denmark, #41 on the Billboard Hot 100
  and #10 on the US Dance Club chart. (Sources: Wikipedia, Wilde Life.)
  - **Credits on _Close_:** keyboards and programming by Ricky Wilde and Tony Swain; guitars by
    Steve Byrd and Ricky Wilde; engineered by James Richards, mixed by Richard Lengyel. The credits
    are album-wide; no drummer and no drum programmer is named, and **no source names the drum
    machine or the synths**. On Kim Wilde's previous album, _Another Step_ (1986), Ricky Wilde is
    credited with **Fairlight III programming**; on _Teases & Dares_ (1984), with a Synclavier
    II, Jupiter-8, DX7, Minimoog and ARP strings.
  - **How it was written (Ricky Wilde, Unsung Heroes podcast, 2020):** alone in the studio on a
    deadline weekend, starting from "a couple of synths" and a groove. He wanted the album's
    version of the Human League's _Dare_, and says the chords are very close to the Human League's
    "Life On Your Own" (1984). The chorus is about his newborn son; Kim finished the lyrics.
  - **Versions:** album 4:33, 7″ edit 3:29, UK 12″ extended version 6:44, and Shep Pettibone
    remixes for the US (12″ 7:35, two dubs).
  - **Hooktheory** has only the chorus: E major, 120 BPM, IV–vii°⁶₄–V–I⁶₄–V.
- **Melody (Hooktheory, TF):** mostly stepwise, ~64% chord tones, 100% diatonic, about one note
  per beat with loose syncopation. The verse sits about a fourth lower than the chorus.

## 5. Generator rules

- **Tempo:** 116–121 BPM, centred on **118.5**, between the two originals.
- **Keys:** minor, weighted toward D minor and E minor.
- **Progression bank:** contains the measured progressions above, each tagged on the style axis
  (0 = TF, 1 = DS). They are varied with the substitutions the songs demonstrate:
  `IV ↔ iv` (the one-note chorus difference) · `v ↔ V` · splitting bar 2 (TF's `VI VII`) ·
  `iv ↔ VI` at the chorus end. Verse progressions can be 8 bars long (DS's two-line verse).
- **Dorian scale:** over IV and ii, melodies raise the 6th. Over a major V they raise the 7th.
- **Form:** intro over the chorus chords → 16-bar verse → 8-bar chorus → a style-dependent
  instrumental block (TF: instrumental chorus with a VI turnaround / DS: interlude + Dorian
  ii–IV turnaround + a 2-bar break) → verse → chorus → a style-dependent bridge (TF: i–IV vamp
  with a melodic-bass solo / DS: interlude block) → optional verse 3 (more likely towards TF) →
  repeated choruses, the last one doubled at the octave → outro (TF: modulate to IV-major and
  vamp / DS: chorus) with a fade.
- **Bass:** the shared figure in every bar the bass plays, with the approach note weighted by
  style ("tf" or "ds", above). Register: TF G1–F♯2, DS A1–G♯2.
  - **Sound:** a saw through a one-pole lowpass (TF brighter), a presence boost around 700 Hz
    (TF +7 dB), a light drive, and a lean lowest octave. Each note holds, then falls linearly.
  - **Mix:** the pad and the sequence are high-passed, and the pad drops its low root, so the
    bass's harmonics stay audible. Measured on Demucs stems of a render, the mid parts had been
    swallowing them.
- **Sequence (TF):** a high 16th pedal on the key's 5th with the tonic or octave on top. It enters
  after the first chorus and plays to the end, in most TF-leaning songs. Each song generates its
  own top-note steps; the original's exact steps are part of the composition.
- **Originals:** `src/references.ts` lays out both songs at their measured form, chords, key,
  tempo, kit and bass figure, playable through the same engine. The lead melodies, riffs and
  melodic bass stay generated: those are the copyrighted compositions.
- **Drums:** four-on-the-floor, snare on 2 and 4, dense humanised 16th hats, fills into new
  sections. TF-leaning songs open a hat on beat 3 of every other bar (MIDI) and often start with
  TF's intro groove: a kick run on beat 4 every other bar, ghost snares after beat 2, a low tom on
  4& in the bars between, and a snare-and-tom fill (MIDI bars 0–3). The tom on 4& continues
  every other bar through the intro.
- **Guitar (TF):** a strum on every 16th, 3–4 strings in G3–G4, sus2 on III and sus4 on IV (the
  MIDI's F as G–C–F, G as G–C–D). It plays in choruses, instrumentals and the outro, never in
  verses. Karplus–Strong strings, overdriven, as Sumner's DI'd guitar.
- **Fills (TF):** into a verse, two staccato bars on VI–VII. The second rests until beat 4, then
  the bass runs root, next root +5, +7, next root (MIDI bars 45–46: C G A → D).
- **Counter-line (TF):** the MIDI's "Synth Lead" is not the vocal. It plays only in the choruses
  (and their last pickup bars), A4–E6 but mostly F5–C6, so it sits above the sung chorus: held
  notes of 6–8 16ths on chord tones, stepping between them, in a 4-bar call and response that
  loops with the chorus chords. On the record (Demucs "other" stem of chorus 1, not the vocal
  stem), it is a plucked electric-piano tone, not a synth lead: held notes strike hard and fade
  about 11 dB over 0.75 s, with harmonics about 5, 11, 17, 22 and 25 dB under the fundamental
  (medians over chorus 1's long notes). The noise between those harmonics is other parts bleeding
  into the stem, not breath: a breathy voice built from that reading sounded wrong. Separated the
  same way, our render with the MIDI's parts puts the line's fundamental a few dB higher against
  the whole mix than the record does, so it isn't quieter there; it still
  sounded a bit quiet by ear, so it plays 3 dB above that. Sound On Sound names the
  session's samplers as an Akai S900 and an Emulator II, not a Fairlight. The generator writes its
  own line in this shape for every chorus, in most TF-leaning songs.

### 6.1 Tonal centre: E major, with the tonic held back

The song is in **E major**, not C♯ minor. Two things make C♯ minor tempting: the hook is A–B, which
in C♯ minor is the ♭VI–♭VII climb that TF and DS use, and the pre-chorus ends on G♯7, the dominant
of C♯ minor. The measurements rule it out:

- **There is no C♯ minor chord anywhere.** The only chord on C♯ is **major**: its E♯ is present
  in both the pad register and the top line, in both pre-choruses.
- **Both secondary dominants resolve the E-major way.** C♯ goes to F♯m (V/ii → ii). G♯7 goes to A
  (V/vi → IV, a deceptive resolution), never to C♯m.
- **E is where the phrases land.** The pre-chorus puts a root-position E on its 3rd and 6th bars,
  and the whole-song key fit gives E major (r 0.66), then A major (0.52); C♯ minor isn't in the
  top six.

What makes the key unusual is how little the tonic sounds. **The bass is on A 42% of the time,
on B 34%, on G♯ 12% and on E only 5%.** The hook is a IV–V loop that touches the tonic only as
inversions in the upper parts (E over B, E over G♯), and the record starts and ends on A. It's a
major key whose home chord is kept for the pre-chorus. That is the reverse of TF and DS, which sit
on their tonic and colour it.

| 2   | B                                              | B major; in beats 3–4 the top line holds E (E over B, a tonic six-four)         | V (I⁶₄)      |
| 3   | A                                              | as bar 1                                                                        | IV           |
| 4   | B, then **G♯** from beat 3 (sometimes from 2&) | B, then E/G♯                                                                    | V → I⁶       |

- **The G♯ on bar 4** comes every 4 bars in the hook, the verses and choruses 1–2, and every 8
  bars in chorus 3 and the outro. In the other 4th bars the bass stays on B.
- **Into a pre-chorus, bar 4 turns to E** (0:44 and 1:32): the bass goes to E on 2&, an 8th
  early, so the I arrives as a push into C♯ (I → V/ii → ii). The stems show G♯ on top with traces
  of B♯ and D♯, which first read as G♯ major over E; by ear (an A/B check against the record),
  plain E major fits and G♯/E clashes. The B♯ is most likely a passing note in the top line,
  leaning into C♯.
- **Once, in chorus 2** (bar 12, 2:12), bar 4 lands on plain E (E over B, then E in the bass) and
  goes back to A. It is the only root-position tonic inside a chorus.
- The pad register holds little more than the roots A and B; the D♯, F♯ and E colours are all in
  the top line. Read as voice-leading, this is Hooktheory's IV–vii°⁶₄–V–I⁶₄–V.

**Pre-chorus** (0:45 and 1:33), one chord per bar:

| Bar     | 1    | 2                         | 3    | 4                     | 5    | 6    | 7         | 8          |
| ------- | ---- | ------------------------- | ---- | --------------------- | ---- | ---- | --------- | ---------- |
| Time    | 0:45 | 0:47                      | 0:49 | 0:51                  | 0:53 | 0:55 | 0:57      | 0:59       |
| Chord   | C♯   | F♯m                       | E    | Bm                    | F♯m  | E    | G♯sus4    | G♯7        |
| Numeral | V/ii | ii                        | I    | **v** (borrowed)      | ii   | I    | V/vi sus4 | V/vi⁷ → IV |
| Bass    | C♯2  | F♯1, F♯2 on beats 2 and 4 | E2   | B1–C♯2–B1–A1–G♯1 walk | F♯1  | E2   | G♯1       | G♯1        |

- **C♯ is major**: its E♯ is clear in both registers. **Bm is minor**: the D natural is as strong as
  the root. So bar 4 borrows the minor v, whose D♮ is the ♭7 of E Mixolydian or E minor. The bass
  walks down from it to the F♯m.
- **G♯sus4 → G♯7:** bar 7 has C♯ and D♯ with no B♯. B♯ and F♯ arrive in bar 8. G♯7 then resolves
  deceptively to the loop's A, not to C♯m. Confirmed by ear: the sus4 fits the record better than
  a plain G♯.

**Other sections:**

- **Intro** (0:00): 2 bars of a high pad on A with only A and E (no third). Then the loop's 4 bars
  without bass (0:05), its 3rd bar coloured A(add9) / Amaj9.
- **Breakdown** (2:22): no bass. Pads on bare A and B, roots and fifths with almost no thirds:
  IV │ V for 8 bars under the lead vocal.
- **Bridge** (2:38): **Amaj7 │ B(add9) │ A/C♯ │ Bsus4–B**, IV │ V │ IV⁶ │ V.
  - The bass plays only pickups: a C♯ on beat 1 and 4& of bar 3, then a syncopated B pedal in
    bar 4.
  - Bar 3 has A, B, C♯ and E over the C♯: **A over C♯** with an added 9th. C♯m7 would need a G♯,
    which the chroma doesn't show. By ear, an A held over the bar fits better than a G♯, though the
    G♯ doesn't clash outright.
- **Break** (3:18): no bass. A(add9) │ B for 8 bars.
- **Ending** (4:23): the loop's A, held until the fade. **The record ends on IV.**

### 6.3 The bass

Neither an octave bass nor a sequenced 16th figure: **legato 8ths on the root**, low and plain,
with one 16th of detail.

```
            1    e    &    a    2    e    &    a    3    e    &    a    4    e    &    a
loop IV     A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   A1
loop V      B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   B1
4th bar     B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    G♯1  -    G♯1  -    G♯1  -    G♯1  -
→ pre       B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E1   -    E1   -      (I, into C♯)
pre ii      F♯1  -    F♯1  -    F♯2  -    F♯1  -    F♯1  -    F♯1  -    F♯2  -    F♯1  -
pre v       B1   -    B1   -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    B1   -    B1   -    A1   -    G♯1  -
```

- **The "4a" note repeats the current root**, a 16th before the change, in about two thirds of the
  loop bars (the share of bars with a clear onset there is 0.53–0.67). Unlike the shared TF/DS
  figure, it doesn't anticipate the next root, and the next root still lands on beat 1.
- **There is no 4a in the pre-chorus.** It has octave hops on its ii bar (F♯2 on beats 2 and 4)
  and a quarter-note walk-down in the v bar.
- **The onsets on the "&"s are stronger than on the beats** (0.8–0.9 against about 0.5): an
  off-beat pump. Part of that may be the kick masking the bass's on-beat attacks in the
  separation.
- **Register: G♯1–F♯2** (52–92 Hz). The loop lives on A1 and B1 (55 and 62 Hz); the pre-chorus
  sits higher, on C♯2 and E2. It is lower than DS (A1–B2) and level with TF's bottom.
- **Timbre:**
  - In the loop, the 2nd harmonic is as loud as the fundamental (−3 to +2 dB; −5 to −8 on the
    pre-chorus's higher notes). The 3rd is 18–20 dB down, and the 4th is louder than the 3rd. That is the signature of two oscillators an octave apart, or
    a sub-oscillator.
  - Harmonics stay within 22 dB of the fundamental only up to 250–740 Hz, so it is **darker than
    TF's bass** (1.7 kHz).
  - Notes are re-struck every 8th with dips of about 5 dB between them: legato, not staccato.
- **Where it plays:** it enters on the last 16th before the hook (0:12). It is silent in the
  intro, the breakdown (2:22, except pickups in the bridge) and the break (3:18). In the 12″ it
  also carries the 0:12 and 2:54 bass-and-drums sections.

### 6.4 The drums

Transcribed from the drum stem with [`scripts/transcribe_drums.py`](../scripts/transcribe_drums.py)
(numbers are shares of a section's bars with a hit on that 16th). The main groove, in the hook,
verses, pre-choruses and choruses:

```
          1    e    &    a    2    e    &    a    3    e    &    a    4    e    &    a
kick      x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .
snare     .    .    .    .    x    .    .    .    .    .    .    .    x    .   (x)   .
hat       x    .    x    o    x    .    x    o    x    .    x    o    x    .    x    o
```

- **Kick on every beat** (1.00), nothing in between.
- **Snare on 2 and 4** (0.88–1.00), with a pickup snare on 4& in a quarter to half of the bars.
- **Hats on the beat, the "&" and the "a", never on the "e"** (0.00 in every groove section).
  The beat and "&" hits are closed (0.7–1.0). The "a" is there in 50–90% of bars, and in about a
  third of them it rings longer (an open hat or a shaker; the stem can't tell which). That gives
  a "tss – tss-tss" skip into each beat. It is lighter than TF and DS, whose hats fill every 16th.
- **Fills:** the last bar of every 8-bar phrase (0:27, 0:43, 0:59 …):

  ```
  kick      x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .    x    .    .    .
  snare     .    .    x    .    x    .    x    .    .    .    x    .    x    .    x    .
  hat       x    .    x    .    x    .    x    .    x    .    x    .    x    .    x    .
  ```

  Snare on every off-beat 8th as well as 2 and 4, and the hats drop to 8ths. The next phrase opens
  with a crash on 1 (the hats skip that beat). There are no toms anywhere.

**The machine** (a guess; no source names it):

- **Kick:** short (its low band lasts about 100 ms), and its pitch falls from about 108 Hz to
  61 Hz in that time.
- **Snare:** no clap-like double attack. Its long, smooth tail is −10 dB at 80 ms and −20 dB at
  220 ms, with no gate cut-off: a plate or hall, not gated reverb.
- **Hats:** crisp and closed. Their top end can't be judged, because the MP3 is cut at 16 kHz.
- **No toms and no pitched percussion.**

That rules out an 808 (its kick rings for 300 ms and more) and a Simmons (no pitched sweeps on
the snare or toms). A sample-based kit fits best, and it is the one thing that is consistent with
the sources: a LinnDrum or Linn 9000, or samples played from Ricky Wilde's Fairlight, which he
programmed on the previous album. A TR-909 kick at a short decay would also match the pitch drop.
Confidence: low.

## 7. What You Came adds to the generator

**Where it sits.** On tempo, form logic, a four-on-the-floor kick and root-8th bass, You Came
belongs with the other two: all three run at 117–120 BPM, open with an instrumental over the hook
chords, and build from 8-bar blocks. It differs on four axes, so it makes the genre a triangle
rather than a line:

| Axis         | TF                                 | DS                                | You Came                                                               |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mode         | minor, Dorian IV, ends in IV major | minor, Aeolian + harmonic-minor V | **major**, tonic held back, ends on IV                                 |
| Hook harmony | i–III–VII–IV                       | i–III–VII–iv                      | **IV–V loop**, the tonic only as I⁶₄ and I⁶                            |
| Bass         | shared figure, next root on 4a     | shared figure, darker             | root 8ths, same root on 4a, darker and octave-layered                  |
| Hats         | dense 16ths                        | dense 16ths                       | **beat, &, a**; 16ths only in the breaks                               |
| Form         | verse ≠ chorus chords              | verse ≠ chorus chords             | **verse and chorus share the loop**; the pre-chorus brings the harmony |

The loop also links the corners. A–B is ♭VI–♭VII in C♯ minor, the same climb TF and DS use in
their verses. You Came lets the climb run forever without landing on the minor tonic.

**New building blocks a three-reference synthpop genre would need:**

1. **Major-key progressions.**
   - **Loop:** IV │ V │ IV │ V–I⁶ (the bass to the 3rd on bar 4), with a top-line voice that
     moves 5→♯4/6 over IV and holds 1 over V. It's the generator's first loop that never sits on
     the tonic.
   - **Pre-chorus:** V/ii │ ii │ I │ v │ ii │ I │ V/vi sus4 │ V/vi⁷, entered from a I pushed an
     8th early. It leaves deceptively to IV.
   - **Substitutions it demonstrates:**
     - the borrowed minor v;
     - the sus4 → 7 delay on a secondary dominant;
     - the deceptive V/vi → IV;
     - the bridge's IVmaj7 │ V(add9) colours.
2. **Form blocks:**
   - a verse over the chorus loop (lead alone) with an 8-bar pre-chorus as the harmonic contrast;
   - a chorus that is the loop plus stacked voices;
   - a breakdown verse with no bass and no snare;
   - a 4-bar bridge into the last choruses;
   - an instrumental with 16th hats;
   - an **ending that holds IV** instead of cadencing.
   - For a 12″ mode, a bass-and-drums intro and a dub block with the snare thinned out.
3. **A bass style:** root 8ths, G♯1–F♯2, with a same-pitch 16th on 4a in loop bars, octave hops on
   ii, and a walk-down from v. The sound is two oscillators an octave apart through a low lowpass (a
   few hundred Hz).
4. **Drum traits:**
   - a "beat-&-a" hat pattern with the "a" sometimes open;
   - a snare-8ths fill every 8 bars, then a crash;
   - per-section muting (kick on 1 only, no snare, 16th hats in breaks);
   - a long, non-gated snare tail.
5. **A riff register:** a syncopated high synth line around C♯6–G♯6, playing in the hook and the
   choruses and resting in the verses. The generator writes its own line; the original's notes are
   the composition.
6. **Keys:** the tempo range stays at 116–121. The key choice gains a major branch (E major here),
   and the style axis becomes two-dimensional: a weight per reference, with tonal centre, loop
   type, bass style and hat pattern read from the weights.
