# Musical analysis: "True Faith" × "The Different Story"

This is the research the generator is built on. It covers New Order's **"True Faith"** (1987) and
Peter Schilling's **"The Different Story (World of Lust and Crime)"** (1989): what the two songs
share, where they differ, and how each finding becomes a rule in the code.

There are two layers of evidence:

1. **Measured from the recordings** (§1–§3): signal analysis of the three files in `inspiration/`
   ("True Faith", the "Different Story" single version, and its 2023 long version). The script is
   [`scripts/analyze_reference.py`](../scripts/analyze_reference.py), and its raw output is
   [`reference-data.json`](reference-data.json). **Where the two layers disagree, the generator
   follows the measurements.**
2. **Written sources** (§4): Hooktheory, Sound On Sound, Wikipedia and a tab site. These cover the
   production facts that audio analysis can't recover (which machines, who played what).

How reliable the measurements are: chords are estimated by matching triad templates against
harmonic-only chroma at half-bar resolution. Passing chords and bars dominated by vocals or riffs
can be misread (for example, the stray "Cm" readings in "True Faith" are most likely C plus an E♭
from the lead lines). The form comes from automatic segmentation plus my reading of the chord
sequences. Timestamps are accurate to about a bar.

### Shared DNA

1. **The same tempo and the same minor engine.** The verse climbs **i → VI → VII → v** in both
   songs, the "epic minor" climb through the two major chords ♭VI and ♭VII. Both verses sit on
   the **minor v**, the melancholy, unresolved dominant.
2. **The same chorus shape, i … VII → IV/iv.** It opens on the tonic, swings through ♭VII and
   lands on the subdominant. The two choruses differ by **a single note**:
   - "True Faith" uses the **major IV** (G–B♮–D, Dorian).
   - "The Different Story" uses the **minor iv** (A–C–E, Aeolian).
3. **Dorian colour in both.** "True Faith" uses the major IV in its chorus, its bridge vamp and
   its whole outro. "The Different Story" uses the Dorian **ii → IV** (F♯m → A) in its
   turnaround. Both brighten the minor key by raising the 6th degree.
4. **The same form logic.** An instrumental intro over the chorus chords, 16-bar verses, 8-bar
   choruses, instrumental sections built from existing progressions, and a final run of repeated
   choruses.
5. **Machine-steady drums.** Four-on-the-floor kick and busy 16th-note hats and sequences.

### Where they differ

- **Ending the verse.** DS turns its second verse line toward the **harmonic-minor major V**
  (B major, with the D♯ leading tone). That gives the continental "Euro" pull back to the tonic.
  TF stays modal the whole time.
- **The outro.** TF's chorus chord **IV (G) becomes the new tonic**: the song modulates up a
  fourth, from minor to major, and vamps there for 32 bars. DS fades on the chorus.
- **The bass** (see the figure below): the same figure in both, from the first bars to the end
  (TF's from 0:08). Only the approach notes differ slightly. Peter Hook's melodic bass guitar
  plays over the top in TF's intro, bridge and outro. From after its first chorus, TF adds a high
  16th sequencer pedal on the key's 5th.

## 4. From the written sources

- **True Faith** was produced by Stephen Hague with New Order, at Advision Studio One.
  - **Drums:** programmed on an **E-mu SP-12**. Stephen Morris overdubbed real hi-hat and cymbals.
  - **Bass:** most bass sounds came from a **DX5**, and the sequenced bass idea came from a
    New York dance track. Peter Hook played the melodic high-register bass through an EHX Clone
    Theory **chorus** pedal, on the intro and outro themes and a bass solo.
  - **Pad:** the harmonic backbone was an **Emulator II + Octave Voyetra-8** pad. Sumner played
    DI'd, overdriven guitar.
  - **Chorus vocals:** two voices sing the chorus in the high octave. (Sources: Sound On Sound,
    Wikipedia.)
- **The Different Story** is Schilling's 1989 English-only single, made in collaboration with
  Michael Cretu (Enigma). It was a top-10 hit in Sweden and reached #16 on the Billboard Dance
  chart.
- **Melody (Hooktheory, TF):** mostly stepwise, ~64% chord tones, 100% diatonic, about one note
  per beat with loose syncopation. The verse sits about a fourth lower than the chorus.

## 5. Generator rules

- **Tempo:** 115–120 BPM, centred on **117.5**.
- **Keys:** minor, weighted toward D minor and E minor.
- **Progression bank:** contains the measured progressions above, each tagged on the style axis
  (0 = TF, 1 = DS). They are varied with the substitutions the songs demonstrate:
  `IV ↔ iv` (the one-note chorus difference) · `v ↔ V` · splitting bar 2 (TF's `VI VII`) ·
  `iv ↔ VI` at the chorus end. Verse progressions can be 8 bars long (DS's two-line verse).
- **Dorian scale:** over IV and ii, melodies raise the 6th. Over a major V they raise the 7th.
- **Form:** intro over the chorus chords → 16-bar verse → 8-bar chorus → a style-dependent
  instrumental block (TF: instrumental chorus with a VI turnaround / DS: interlude + Dorian
  ii–IV turnaround + a 2-bar break) → verse → chorus → a style-dependent bridge (TF: i–IV vamp
  with a melodic-bass solo / DS: interlude block) → optional verse 3 (more likely towards TF) →
  repeated choruses, the last one doubled at the octave → outro (TF: modulate to IV-major and
  vamp / DS: chorus) with a fade.
- **Bass:** the shared figure in every bar the bass plays, with the approach note weighted by
  style ("tf" or "ds", above). Register: TF G1–F♯2, DS A1–G♯2.
  - **Sound:** a saw through a one-pole lowpass (TF brighter), a presence boost around 700 Hz
    (TF +7 dB), a light drive, and a lean lowest octave. Each note holds, then falls linearly.
  - **Mix:** the pad and the sequence are high-passed, and the pad drops its low root, so the
    bass's harmonics stay audible. Measured on Demucs stems of a render, the mid parts had been
    swallowing them.
- **Sequence (TF):** a high 16th pedal on the key's 5th with the tonic or octave on top. It enters
  after the first chorus and plays to the end, in most TF-leaning songs. Each song generates its
  own top-note steps; the original's exact steps are part of the composition.
- **Originals:** `src/references.ts` lays out both songs at their measured form, chords, key,
  tempo, kit and bass figure, playable through the same engine. The lead melodies, riffs and
  melodic bass stay generated: those are the copyrighted compositions.
- **Drums:** four-on-the-floor, snare on 2 and 4, dense humanised 16th hats, fills into new
  sections. TF-leaning songs open a hat on beat 3 of every other bar (MIDI) and often start with
  TF's intro groove: a kick run on beat 4 every other bar, ghost snares after beat 2, a low tom on
  4& in the bars between, and a snare-and-tom fill (MIDI bars 0–3). The tom on 4& continues
  every other bar through the intro.
- **Guitar (TF):** a strum on every 16th, 3–4 strings in G3–G4, sus2 on III and sus4 on IV (the
  MIDI's F as G–C–F, G as G–C–D). It plays in choruses, instrumentals and the outro, never in
  verses. Karplus–Strong strings, overdriven, as Sumner's DI'd guitar.
- **Fills (TF):** into a verse, two staccato bars on VI–VII. The second rests until beat 4, then
  the bass runs root, next root +5, +7, next root (MIDI bars 45–46: C G A → D).
- **Counter-line (TF):** the MIDI's "Synth Lead" is not the vocal. It plays only in the choruses
  (and their last pickup bars), A4–E6 but mostly F5–C6, so it sits above the sung chorus: held
  notes of 6–8 16ths on chord tones, stepping between them, in a 4-bar call and response that
  loops with the chorus chords. On the record (Demucs "other" stem of chorus 1, not the vocal
  stem), it is a soft, sampled-sounding wind voice, not a synth lead. Its harmonics fall about
  10, 17, 18 and 29 dB under the fundamental, breath noise carries much of it, notes swell in over
  50–90 ms, and its pitch wanders 5–15 cents with no regular vibrato. Sound On Sound names the
  session's samplers as an Akai S900 and an Emulator II, not a Fairlight. The generator writes its
  own line in this shape for every chorus, in most TF-leaning songs.
