# Musical analysis: "True Faith" × "The Different Story"

This is the research the generator is built on. It covers New Order's **"True Faith"** (1987) and
Peter Schilling's **"The Different Story (World of Lust and Crime)"** (1989): what the two songs
share, where they differ, and how each finding becomes a rule in the code.

There are two layers of evidence:

1. **Measured from the recordings** (§1–§3): signal analysis of the three files in `inspiration/`
   ("True Faith", the "Different Story" single version, and its 2023 long version). The script is
   [`scripts/analyze_reference.py`](../scripts/analyze_reference.py), and its raw output is
   [`reference-data.json`](reference-data.json). **Where the two layers disagree, the generator
   follows the measurements.**
2. **Written sources** (§4): Hooktheory, Sound On Sound, Wikipedia and a tab site. These cover the
   production facts that audio analysis can't recover (which machines, who played what).

How reliable the measurements are: chords are estimated by matching triad templates against
harmonic-only chroma at half-bar resolution. Passing chords and bars dominated by vocals or riffs
can be misread (for example, the stray "Cm" readings in "True Faith" are most likely C plus an E♭
from the lead lines). The form comes from automatic segmentation plus my reading of the chord
sequences. Timestamps are accurate to about a bar.

### Shared DNA

1. **The same tempo and the same minor engine.** The verse climbs **i → VI → VII → v** in both
   songs, the "epic minor" climb through the two major chords ♭VI and ♭VII. Both verses sit on
   the **minor v**, the melancholy, unresolved dominant.
2. **The same chorus shape, i … VII → IV/iv.** It opens on the tonic, swings through ♭VII and
   lands on the subdominant. The two choruses differ by **a single note**:
   - "True Faith" uses the **major IV** (G–B♮–D, Dorian).
   - "The Different Story" uses the **minor iv** (A–C–E, Aeolian).
3. **Dorian colour in both.** "True Faith" uses the major IV in its chorus, its bridge vamp and
   its whole outro. "The Different Story" uses the Dorian **ii → IV** (F♯m → A) in its
   turnaround. Both brighten the minor key by raising the 6th degree.
4. **The same form logic.** An instrumental intro over the chorus chords, 16-bar verses, 8-bar
   choruses, instrumental sections built from existing progressions, and a final run of repeated
   choruses.
5. **Machine-steady drums.** Four-on-the-floor kick and busy 16th-note hats and sequences.

### Where they differ

- **Ending the verse.** DS turns its second verse line toward the **harmonic-minor major V**
  (B major, with the D♯ leading tone). That gives the continental "Euro" pull back to the tonic.
  TF stays modal the whole time.
- **The outro.** TF's chorus chord **IV (G) becomes the new tonic**: the song modulates up a
  fourth, from minor to major, and vamps there for 27 bars. DS fades on the chorus.
- **The bass.** DS plays Hi-NRG/Italo-style root 16ths that move to each new chord one 16th
  early. TF's bass "bounces" with upper neighbour notes. Peter Hook's melodic bass guitar plays
  over the top.

## 4. From the written sources

- **True Faith** was produced by Stephen Hague with New Order, at Advision Studio One.
  - **Drums:** programmed on an **E-mu SP-12**. Stephen Morris overdubbed real hi-hat and cymbals.
  - **Bass:** most bass sounds came from a **DX5**, and the sequenced bass idea came from a
    New York dance track. Peter Hook played the melodic high-register bass through an EHX Clone
    Theory **chorus** pedal, on the intro and outro themes and a bass solo.
  - **Pad:** the harmonic backbone was an **Emulator II + Octave Voyetra-8** pad. Sumner played
    DI'd, overdriven guitar.
  - **Chorus vocals:** two voices sing the chorus in the high octave. (Sources: Sound On Sound,
    Wikipedia.)
- **The Different Story** is Schilling's 1989 English-only single, made in collaboration with
  Michael Cretu (Enigma). It was a top-10 hit in Sweden and reached #16 on the Billboard Dance
  chart.
- **Melody (Hooktheory, TF):** mostly stepwise, ~64% chord tones, 100% diatonic, about one note
  per beat with loose syncopation. The verse sits about a fourth lower than the chorus.
