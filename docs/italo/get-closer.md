No chorus key change: the expected Italo habit of lifting the last chorus doesn't happen here.

## 5. The bass

**Not an octave bass.** Get Closer's bass is **straight 8ths on the root**, every 8th struck, with
no octave jumps, no 16ths, no approach notes and no anticipation.

```
            1    e    &    a    2    e    &    a    3    e    &    a    4    e    &    a
loop i      E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -    E2   -
loop IV     A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -    A1   -
loop ♭VII   D2   -    D2   -    D2   -    D2   -    D2   -    D2   -    D2   -    D2   -
verse IV⁶   C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -    C♯2  -
chorus V    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -    B1   -
```

- **Octaves, checked directly.** In the bass stem the fundamental band (E2, 82 Hz, for an E bar) is
  loud on every 8th, and the band an octave below (E1) stays 25–35 dB under it. The octave above
  (E3) follows the fundamental note for note, as a harmonic would; it never alternates with it. The
  same holds for A1, B1 and D2.
- **Every 8th is struck equally.** The onset strength is 0.6–0.8 on both the beats and the "&"s, and
  0.03–0.05 on the "e" and "a". The "&"s are 1–2 dB louder, likely because the kick masks the
  on-beat attacks in the separation.
- **Articulation:** each note starts at full level and decays steadily, about 16 dB in the 240 ms
  before the next 8th: a plucked envelope that fills the 8th, not a gated staccato and not legato.
- **Register: A1–E2** (55–82 Hz, with the tuning offset). The lowest note is the IV's A1 and the
  highest is the tonic's E2, so the line leaps down a 5th to the IV and moves a step at a time
  otherwise (E–D, D–E, E–C♯–D in the verse, E–D–B–E in the chorus).
- **Timbre:** dark. The 2nd harmonic is about as loud as the fundamental (0 to −6 dB), the 3rd is
  9–15 dB down, and harmonics stay within 22 dB of the fundamental only up to 190–335 Hz. That's
  darker than You Came's bass (250–740 Hz) and far darker than TF's open saw (1.7 kHz).
- **The same in every section.** The pattern doesn't change between intro, verse, chorus, outro or
  the 12″ blocks. The bass only stops for the 12″'s break (4:18–4:50) and re-enters on 4 of the
  break's last bar.

  cymbal: a crash (?). The 12″'s stop block and hook reprise keep the pickups but not the hit.

**The machine** (no source names it):

- **Kick:** very short. Its 30–120 Hz band falls 22 dB in 50 ms and 47 dB in 100 ms, and its pitch
  falls from about 54 Hz to 32 Hz within the first 50 ms. That rules out an 808, whose kick rings
  for 300 ms and more.
- **Snare:** a broadband noise hit (1 to over 10 kHz) with little tonal body. The 150–300 Hz body is
  gone (−30 dB) by 100 ms, but the noise is still at −13 dB at 100 ms and −26 dB at 300 ms: a long
  noise tail, a plate reverb or a long-decay noise snare. At 2 ms resolution its attack shows dips
  and re-attacks over the first 20 ms, but no cleaner than the ripple later in the tail, so a
  layered clap can't be confirmed or ruled out from the stem (?).
- **Hats:** closed and short (−22 dB at 50 ms).
- **Machine:** a short, pitched-down kick with a long noisy snare and plain 8th hats fits a
  sample-based machine of 1984 (a LinnDrum or an Oberheim DMX) better than a Roland analogue box.
  Confidence: low.

## 8. Written sources

**Credits** (Discogs, the Italian 12″ on Merak Music, MKX 700, 1984, and the German ZYX pressings):

- **Music: P. & R. Nicolosi. Lyrics: B. (Barbara) Addoms.** Published by Sangy Productions.
- **Produced, arranged, recorded and mixed by L. (Lino) Nicolosi and R. (Roberto) Gasparini**, at

  herself).
- **Versions:** the Merak 12″ has the Vocal Version (6:16) and the Instrumental Version (6:02); its
  labels print 6:00 and 5:30. The ZYX 12″ (5187) has 6:12 and 5:58, and the ZYX 7″ (1107) has
  3:40 vocal and instrumental sides.

**Context:**

- **Wikipedia** gives 1984 as the release year and lists chart peaks of #12 in Germany, #12 in
  Italy, #11 in Switzerland and #33 in France. Roberto Gasparini discovered Monica Stucchi and gave
  her the name Valerie Dore. Lino Nicolosi, unsure of her voice, used session singers (his then-wife
  Dora Carofiglio and his sister Rossana Nicolosi) on some tracks, including "The Night".
- **An Italian fan site** (gli anni 80) goes further: Stucchi was the face of the project and
  Carofiglio sang all the singles, which would include "Get Closer".
- **Resident Advisor**, reviewing the 2017 Running Back reissue (with a Gerd Janson edit and a Tiger
  & Woods remix), calls it a slow, romantic Italo disco record.

**"The Night" was made by the same team.** Wikipedia credits it to Giuseppe Nicolosi and Barbara
Addoms (writers) and to Lino Nicolosi and Roberto Gasparini (producers), on Merak in 1984. Only the
composer credit is spelled differently ("P. & R. Nicolosi" on Get Closer); "Pino" is the usual
short form of Giuseppe, so this is likely the same writer plus Rossana Nicolosi, but no source says
so.

| Axis    | TF / DS (synthpop)                           | You Came (synthpop)             | Get Closer                                                                                  |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tempo   | 117–120                                      | 119                             | **112** (and "The Night" 110)                                                               |
| Harmony | i–III–VII–IV / iv, one chord per bar         | IV–V loop, major                | **pads i–♭VII–IV–♭VI over a bass i–i–IV–♭VII**: slash chords D/E and C/D; chorus i–i–♭VII–V |
| Bass    | shared figure, 4e/4a hiccup and anticipation | root 8ths, same root on 4a      | **plain root 8ths**, plucked decay, A1–E2, no detail at all                                 |
| Hats    | dense 16ths                                  | beat, &, a                      | **8ths only**; 16ths only inside fills                                                      |
| Form    | intro, verse, chorus, instrumental, bridge   | loop shared by verse and chorus | **16-bar blocks only**, no pre-chorus or bridge; a 12″ that inserts 64 instrumental bars    |

**New building blocks it needs:**

1. **A two-layer loop.** The pads and the bass play different progressions, and the slash chords
   fall out of that: pads **i │ ♭VII │ IV │ ♭VI**, bass **i │ i │ IV │ ♭VII**. A generator that
   writes the bass from the chord roots can't produce it; the bass needs its own root sequence per
   loop. With the bass muted (the 12″ break), the pads' progression stands alone.
2. **Modal mixture in one loop:** the Dorian IV (A major) and the Aeolian ♭VI (C major) two bars
   apart, so C♯ and C♮ alternate. Plus the harmonic-minor V (B major) in the chorus, entered through
   a V¹¹ (A/B) half-bar.
3. **A bass walk by substitution:** IV → IV⁶ (A/C♯) in some loop bars, giving E–C♯–D.
4. **The "Italo 8ths" bass style:** root 8ths, every one re-struck, a plucked decay of about 16 dB
   per 8th, dark (harmonics within 22 dB only to about 300 Hz), A1–E2, constant across sections.
   It's simpler than any synthpop figure. **It isn't the octave bass**; that building block has to
   come from the other Italo references, if they have it.
5. **Drum traits:** 8th hats with nothing on the "e" and "a"; a beat-long noise hit (crash?) every 4
   bars in the intro and hook with a 4&–4a pickup before it; snare-8th fills on the 16th bar of each
   chorus; a short, pitched-down kick; a long noisy snare.
6. **12″ form blocks (issue #2):** all made from the song's own loop with parts muted, in 8- and
   16-bar blocks, inserted between the second and third chorus:
   - a **stop block** (upper parts on 2 bars of every 4, bass and drums throughout);
   - a **hook reprise** (the instrumental hook again);
   - a **fill block** (the verse backing with a fill every other bar);
   - a **bass-less break** (pads and drums only, fills in the second half);
   - then a chorus and a longer outro.
7. **A 16th-note line** in D4–B4 for the hook, built from 3-note scale cells (the generator writes
   its own), and a countervoice in 8ths for the choruses and outro. §10 has both parts' rhythm,
   register and patch.

**Generic Italo, or specific to this song?** With only this record measured in depth, the split is a
working guess for the genre synthesis to confirm against "The Night" and "Faces":

- **Probably generic Italo:** the 110–112 BPM tempo, the straight 4/4 kit with 8th hats, a root-8th
  bass with no figure, a minor key, instrumental hook sections with a 16th synth line, and a 6-minute
  12″ that inserts long instrumental blocks made by muting.
- **Probably specific to Get Closer:** the two-layer loop (i–♭VII–IV–♭VI over i–i–IV–♭VII), the
  IV⁶ walk, the stop block, and the B-major V in the chorus.

**How close it is to "The Night".** The sources say same writers, same producers, same label, same
year and probably the same singer. A first look at The Night's own measurements (its automatic
analysis in the parallel session, and a glance at its bass stem; `the-night.md` has the full
picture) agrees on the outline:

- **Tempo:** The Night runs at 110.2 BPM, Get Closer at 111.8.
- **Key and tuning:** The Night is in F minor but about 40 cents flat, while Get Closer is in E minor
  and about 28 cents sharp. On the tape the two tonics are only about a third of a semitone apart.
  Both records sit between concert keys.
- **Loop:** The Night's is a 4-bar minor loop with a ♭VII (read automatically as Fm │ Fm │ Cm │ B♭).
- **Bass:** The Night's bass also holds roots in 8ths, with at least one octave jump.

So the two Valerie Dore records are near-twins in tempo, kit and bass approach, and a generator can
treat them as one "Nicolosi/Gasparini" corner of the Italo genre. Their harmony differs: Get Closer
has the slash-chord loop, and The Night has a different minor loop.

## 10. Synth parts for the generator

The two synth lines that carry the record besides the pads: the hook's 16th line and the chorus
countervoice. A generated version (seed 7, the Get Closer corner) had the hook's timbre wrong and no
countervoice in the chorus.

**How they were measured.** Neither line has a stem of its own: both sit in Demucs's "other" stem
with the pads, and a six-stem split (htdemucs_6s) left its piano and guitar stems empty. So:

- **Hook line:** the pads were cancelled. The intro plays the same 4-bar pad loop with no line, so
  the intro's magnitude spectrum, 16th by 16th on the drifting grid, was subtracted from each hook
  bar. What's left is mostly the line (the hook bars lose only 1–3 dB), and four repeats of the
  cycle were averaged.
- **Countervoice:** each candidate note was traced 16th by 16th through the same bar of every chorus
  cycle (15 bars across choruses 1–3 and the 12″ outro), and its entries were read where its level
  jumps. Its harmonics were read on its A and B in the C/E bar, where neither note is in the pads.

Bars and times are the 12″'s; uncertain readings carry "(?)" until the ear check.

### The hook line

**Register: D4–B4, not C5–E6.** The draft of §7 had the line an octave too high; it was reading the
2nd harmonic. Odd harmonics as strong as the fundamental confirm the lower octave. The line sits in
the pads' own register (pads from about C4 up), which is why it needs a cutting timbre.

**Pattern.** Two 16-note phrases per 4-bar loop, each a 3-note scale cell repeated across the beat
(3 against 4), with rests between. The record's notes, for reference (each song writes its own):

| Loop bar (pads / bass) | Beats 1–2                     | Beats 3–4                          |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 1 (Em / E)             | rest                          | G F♯ E D │ F♯(?) D E D: a run down |
| 2 (D / E)              | D E D · D E D · D E: the cell | rest                               |
| 3 (A / A)              | rest                          | rest on beat 3, then G A B G       |
| 4 (C / D)              | A B G · A B G · A B           | G A B G, then rest on beat 4       |

- **Phrase 1** starts on beat 3 of bar 1: a scale run down, then the D–E–D cell.
- **Phrase 2** starts on beat 4 of bar 3 and is the G–A–B cell five times over.
- Both phrases cross the bar line. They move by steps: no triad leaps and no octave jumps.
- Beats 1–2 of the odd bars and beats 3–4 of the even bars are empty, so the line answers itself
  every two bars instead of filling every beat.
- It plays only in the hook (0:18) and the 12″ hook reprise (3:27), the same notes both times. It
  doesn't play in the verses, choruses or the break.

**Timbre:**

- **Source: a narrow pulse.** Measured from the fundamental, harmonics 2–4 sit at −3.5 to +2 dB,
  the 5th at −7, the 6th at −12 and the 8th–11th at −16 to −20 dB. A pulse of about 12% width
  through a 12 dB low-pass at about 3 kHz (Q 2) fits this best: 6 dB RMS error, against 8 dB for the
  best resonant saw and much worse for a square, whose even harmonics are missing.
- **Filter envelope:** harmonics 2–4 lose 4–9 dB against the fundamental within the first 100 ms of
  a note. That's a short sweep down, about an octave, settling around 2.4 kHz.
- **Amp envelope:** an attack under 5 ms. The note falls 7–12 dB across its 16th (134 ms). The last
  note of a phrase is cut at about 115 ms and is gone within 30 ms, so it's gated, not left to
  ring.
- **Stereo and effects:** as wide as the pads. In the hook band the left/right correlation is 0.09,
  against −0.10 for the pads alone, though the line is 6–7 dB louder than them. That points to a
  stereo chorus or ensemble. There's no audible echo: the rests after each phrase stay more than
  20 dB down.
- **Level:** the synth stem rises 6.7 dB from the intro to the hook. The line sits about 6 dB over
  the pads.

**Patch** (the riff track; this replaces the DX bell and saw pluck for Get Closer):

```ts
new NativeMonoSynth({
  // A 12% pulse: |sin(π·n·0.12)|/n, harmonics 1–16.
  oscillator: {
    type: "square",
    partials: [
      1, 0.93, 0.82, 0.68, 0.52, 0.35, 0.19, 0.04, 0.08, 0.16, 0.21, 0.22, 0.21, 0.16, 0.11, 0.04,
    ],
  },
  filter: { type: "lowpass", rolloff: -12, Q: 2 },
  envelope: { attack: 0.003, decay: 0.25, sustain: 0.25, release: 0.04 },
  filterEnvelope: {
    attack: 0.002,
    decay: 0.15,
    sustain: 0.4,
    release: 0.05,
    baseFrequency: 1800,
    octaves: 1,
  },
  maxPolyphony: 4,
}).chain(
  new Tone.Chorus({ frequency: 0.8, delayTime: 3.5, depth: 0.5, wet: 0.5 }).start(),
  riffChannel,
);
```

- **Gate:** 90% of a 16th.
- **New:** the chorus on the riff channel. The chorus rate and depth weren't measured; only the
  width was.
- **What the generator should change** (`generateLine16`, riff base MIDI 72):
  - move the line down an octave, to about MIDI 62–71;
  - write two 16-note phrases per 4 bars from 3-note scale cells, starting on beat 3 and beat 4,
    instead of 8ths in beats 1–2 and 16ths in beats 3–4 of every bar.

### The chorus

**What makes it the chorus.** Measured against the verse:

| Layer        | Verse                                   | Chorus                                                                   |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Drums        | the groove, fill on bar 16              | the same groove; the bar-16 fill is snare 8ths                           |
| Bass         | root 8ths                               | root 8ths (E E D B)                                                      |
| Pads         | the loop, from about C4 up              | the chorus chords: Em │ C/E │ D │ A/B → B                                |
| Countervoice | none                                    | **8ths from beat 2, every bar**, heard as C6–B6, near-mono in the centre |
| Hook line    | none                                    | none                                                                     |
| Voice        | G3–A4                                   | D4–B4                                                                    |
| Synth stem   | −31.2 dB RMS (verse 1), −25.7 (verse 2) | −22.3 (chorus 1), −22.8 (chorus 2): 3–9 dB up; 2–8 kHz rise 10–15 dB     |

The lift comes from the countervoice and the higher voice. There's no new drum part, no 16th part
and no extra pad layer. The countervoice also narrows the top of the mix: the 1.5–6 kHz band's
left/right correlation goes from 0.21 in the verse to 0.62 in the chorus and 0.86 in the outro.

**Chorus bar 2 is C/E.** In every chorus bar 2, the pads hold C5, G5, C6 and E6 over the E bass, and
the countervoice plays C E G A B. So the bar is ♭VI over the tonic bass, not i. This settles the
§4 candidate (clip 4b) on the data, pending the ear check.

**What the generated chorus gets wrong.** From the code on `main` (genres.ts `italo`, composer.ts
`planItalo`, arranger.ts), not from a measurement of the render:

1. **No countervoice.** The Italo chorus plays drums, bass, pads, the chorus lead and, half the
   time, the arp; `counter` is off for the genre.
2. **Bar 2 is i.** The bank's Get Closer chorus is i │ i │ ♭VII │ IV/V → V; the record's bar 2 is
   ♭VI/1 (C/E).
3. **A 16th arpeggio in about half the choruses** (`arp: rng.chance(0.5)`). The record has no 16th
   part in its choruses; their motion is the countervoice's 8ths.
4. **The same pads as the verse.** The Italo pads are The Night's octave-stacked pads (`octaves`,
   a triad from D4 and another from E5) at the same velocity in the verse and the chorus. That makes
   the generated verse as bright as its chorus. In the record the brightness is the countervoice's,
   and it arrives with the chorus.

### The chorus countervoice

**Where it plays.** Every bar of all three choruses and of the outro (a chorus to fade); nowhere
else. Checked on its A and B entries in each cycle's C/E bar: they rise 7–30 dB in every chorus and
outro cycle and not at all in the hook, the verses, the hook reprise or the break.

**Rhythm.** A rest on beat 1, then 8ths on 2, 2&, 3, 3&, 4 and 4&. Each note rings for about a
quarter, so each overlaps the next.

**Notes.** Up the chord from beat 2 (root, 3rd, 5th), then back down, or on up through the 6th and
7th over C. The last 8th of a bar is a tone of the next chord: E into A, B into Em. Read as the
median of the 15 bars:

| Chord   | 2   | 2&  | 3      | 3&  | 4      | 4&     |
| ------- | --- | --- | ------ | --- | ------ | ------ |
| Em      | E   | G   | B (?)  | G   | E      | (held) |
| C/E     | C   | E   | G      | A   | B      | A      |
| D       | D   | F♯  | A      | F♯  | (held) | E      |
| A/B → B | E   | A   | D♯ (?) | (?) | F♯     | B      |

It's the "follows the chords" line: a chord arpeggio in 8ths, not a tune. The generator can write
it from each bar's chord: the root on beat 2, up through the triad, turning back or adding the 6th
and 7th, with the last note a tone of the next chord.

**Register.** The fundamentals are C5–B5, but the octave above is 4–11 dB louder, so the line is
heard as C6–B6 (?).

**Timbre:**

- **Spectrum:** measured from the lower fundamental, the octave is +4 dB (on A) to +11 dB (on B),
  the 3rd harmonic −8 to −11 dB, the 4th and 5th about −10 dB and the 6th −15 dB. Little lies
  above 5 kHz. It's a soft, octave-doubled tone, like two near-sine or triangle oscillators at 8′
  and 4′ with the 4′ louder, or a flute-like organ register.
- **Envelope:** a rise of 20 dB in about 30 ms, settling 8–10 dB down by 250 ms and holding there.
  Notes overlap by about an 8th, so the release is about 0.3 s.
- **Stereo and effects:** near-mono and centred; no chorus. No echo was found.
- **Level:** its entries reach about the level of the pads' top notes.

**Patch** (a counter track for Italo; Get Closer's notes go in at C5–B5, and the partials add the
louder octave):

```ts
new NativeSynth({
  oscillator: { type: "sine", partials: [1, 1.8, 0.28, 0.3, 0.3, 0.15] },
  envelope: { attack: 0.025, decay: 0.25, sustain: 0.35, release: 0.35 },
  maxPolyphony: 4,
}).connect(counterChannel);
```

- **Notes:** a quarter long, on the 8th grid, so they overlap.
- **Role in the arrangement:** on in every chorus and in the chorus-to-fade outro, off everywhere
  else.
- **Fits the existing tracks:** the `counter` channel and `addCounter`'s rule (chorus, plus an
  outro that is a chorus) already match this. Italo needs the odds turned on for Get Closer and a
  writer for this arpeggio in place of TF's counter-line. TF's patch, an FM electric piano, is a
  different sound.
