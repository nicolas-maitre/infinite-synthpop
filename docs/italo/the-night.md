## 3. Tonal centre: F minor, Dorian, with a relative-major chorus

The song is in **F minor**:

- The bass sits on F 40–43% of the time, and every intro and pedal block is an F pedal.
- The whole-song key fit gives F minor (r 0.66 original, 0.73 instrumental), well clear of the
  next candidate (F major 0.46, E♭ major 0.48).
- The verse loop starts on Fm and every return to the verse lands on it (E♭ → Fm, VII → i).

The verse is **Dorian**: its IV is B♭ **major**, with D natural held across three octaves in the
pad (D4, D5, D6). Its v is Cm7, minor, with no E natural. The only clear E natural is the bass's
passing E1 on beat 4 of bar 4, a leading tone into the drop.

The chorus is the question. Its loop is A♭ │ D♭/F │ G♭ │ E♭7sus4 → E♭, and the E♭ chord carries a
D♭ (its 7th). Heard in A♭ major that is **I │ IV⁶ │ ♭VII │ V7sus4 → V**, a dominant cadence back
into A♭ every 4 bars: the chorus tonicises the relative major. In F minor the same chords are
III │ VI⁶ │ ♭II │ VII. The G♭ is the one chord outside F Aeolian or Dorian: a Neapolitan ♭II in F
minor, or a borrowed ♭VII in A♭. The record never cadences in A♭ for good; the last chorus fades on
the loop, and every chorus–verse join goes E♭ → Fm.

**Verdict: F minor (Dorian verse), with the chorus moving to the relative major for its 16 bars.**
It is the reverse of You Came, which kept its tonic back; here the tonic is a 40% pedal, and the
chorus is where it lets go. The automatic "G♯ major" and "A♯ minor" readings in the written sources
(§8) come from the untuned A♭ chorus and from the 42-cent flat tuning.

## 5. The bass

**Not the classic octave bass.** There is no 8th-by-8th alternation between the root and its
octave. The bass plays **legato root 8ths** ("disco 8ths": a note on every beat and every "&", none
on the "e" or "a"), accented on the off-beats, and moves up the octave for 2–6 8ths at a time in
fixed places.

How this was checked: for every 8th, the level at the root's fundamental was compared with the
level at its octave. In an octave bass the two swap every 8th. Here they stay level (within 3 dB,
H2 − H1 between −3 and +2 dB on every 8th of the bar, averaged over the verse); the octave stretches
show up as the fundamental dropping 15–30 dB for a few 8ths, then coming back.

```
              1    e    &    a    2    e    &    a    3    e    &    a    4    e    &    a
verse F       F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -
lifted F      F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F2   -    F2   -    F2   -    F2   -    F1   -     (bars 23, 113)
verse C       C2   -    C2   -    C2   -    C2   -    C2   -    C2   -    C2   -    C2   -
verse B♭      B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -    B♭1  -
chorus F→G♭   F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F1   -    F2   -    F2   -     (into G♭2)
chorus G♭     G♭2  -    G♭2  -    G♭2  -    G♭1  -    G♭1  -    G♭1  -    G♭1  -    G♭1  -
chorus E♭     E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -    E♭1  -
```

- **Every 8th is struck, the off-beats harder.** The average 8th rises to its peak in 30–40 ms
  and decays about 13 dB before the next one, so the line pumps without gaps. In the bass stem the
  "&" notes attack 2–6 dB harder than the on-beat ones in the 3rd–5th harmonics, and in the full
  mix the kick covers the on-beat attacks, so what you hear is a bass that **hits on the
  off-beats** against the kick on the beats. By ear, the user hears the off-beats as the sequenced
  bass. It is the off-beat feel of the Italo octave bass, made with one pitch instead of two.
- **The octave lifts are part of the pattern, not fills**, and they repeat exactly wherever a
  section repeats:
  - in the synth-lead sections, the first F bar of every loop goes up the octave for 2–4 8ths around beats 2–3
    (bars 23, 27, 31, 35 and their repeats); the sung verse doesn't lift there;
  - the second F bar often ends with 2–3 8ths up, leading into the C;
  - the C bar occasionally lifts around beats 3–4 (C3 in bars 29 and 61);
  - in the first loop of each chorus, the F bar ends up the octave and carries into the G♭ bar,
    which plays G♭2 for its first 3–4 8ths and drops to G♭1 (bars 40–41, 72–73, 130–131).
- **No anticipation, no 16ths.** Every root change lands on beat 1, and nothing falls on the "e"
  or "a" (the automatic "B1" passing notes in some C bars are a pitch-tracker artefact: there is no
  energy a semitone below the root there).
- **Register: E♭1–G♭2** (39–92 Hz), with C3 as an occasional top. It is lower than You Came's
  G♯1–F♯2 because the chorus reaches E♭1. The verse lives on F1, C2 and B♭1 (43–65 Hz).
- **Timbre:** very dark. The 2nd harmonic is level with the fundamental (+1 to −7 dB): an octave
  layer, as in You Came. The 3rd is 11–20 dB down, and nothing above the 4th–6th harmonic is within
  22 dB of the fundamental, so the content stops at 150–390 Hz.
- **Where it plays:** everywhere except the two 2-bar drops (bars 5–6, 95–96) and the
  instrumental's 16-bar break. In the intro it enters with the drums on the first beat, and in bar
  4 it walks F–F–F–E1 into the drop.
- **The instrumental** carries the same bass part. Its bass stem reads the octave (F2, C3) far
  more often, for whole half-bars (inst 0:09–0:13 is almost all F2), but by ear the two stems
  sound the same: the instrumental's mix just has less of the fundamental.

**The machine** (no source names it):

- **Kick:** an analog-style pitch sweep, 97 Hz at the attack, 71 Hz at 20 ms, 54 Hz at 40 ms,
  42 Hz at 70 ms, settling at 36 Hz; 20 dB down by 110 ms and gone by 170 ms. Short and very deep.
- **Backbeat:** the 1–4 kHz envelope has 5–6 bursts 2–8 ms apart in its first 25 ms, the shape of

  20 dB down at 114 ms and 30 dB at 230 ms: a short room or plate, no gated reverb.

That points to an **analog Roland machine (TR-808 or TR-909), or a synthesised kick with an 808 or
909 clap**, rather than the LinnDrum or Oberheim DMX the brief expected: their kicks are samples
with little pitch sweep and don't fall to 36 Hz. The multi-burst clap fits the 808 and 909 (and
also the LinnDrum's clap, which alone doesn't decide it). Confidence: low.

## 8. Written sources, and corrections

**What the sources say:**

- **Release:** 1984, Valerie Dore's debut single. Italian label Merak Music (12″ MK 100); German
  releases on ZYX. 80sneverend says it was recorded in late 1983 and grew through Italian TV
  appearances in 1984.
- **Credits:** written by Giuseppe Nicolosi and Barbara Addoms; produced by Lino Nicolosi and
  Roberto Gasparini; arranged by Lino Nicolosi (Wikipedia). The Nicolosi brothers also ran the band
  Novecento.
- **The voice:** Valerie Dore was the stage persona of Monica Stucchi, but the record's voice is
  **Dora Carofiglio**, Novecento's singer and Lino Nicolosi's then wife (Wikipedia; 80sneverend).

## 9. What The Night adds to the generator

**Where it sits.**

|          | You Came (§6 of analysis.md)      | The Night                                                                        |
| -------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tempo    | 119.0                             | **110.3**, 9 BPM slower than any synthpop reference                              |
| Tonic    | held back (E 5% of the bass)      | a pedal (F 43%)                                                                  |
| Loops    | one loop for verse and chorus     | **two loops**: verse i–♭VII/i–v7–IV, chorus III–VI⁶–♭II–VII7 (A♭: I–IV⁶–♭VII–V7) |
| Contrast | the pre-chorus brings the harmony | the chorus brings the harmony (a move to the relative major)                     |
| Hats     | beat, &, a                        | quiet 8ths; an open off-beat hat only in the chorus                              |
| Fills    | a snare-8ths fill every 8 bars    | **none in 5:46**                                                                 |
| Bass     | root 8ths, 4a re-strike           | root 8ths accented on the off-beats, octave lifts in fixed places                |

Against the three synthpop references it is slower (110 against 117–120), more static (one drum bar for the whole record), and harmonically richer in the chorus. It shares the
four-on-the-floor kick, the root-8th bass and the 4-bar loop logic. What reads as "Italo" in it is
less any single device than the combination: a machine groove that never changes, pads in stacked
octaves, a 16th arpeggio, a minor verse over a tonic pedal and a relative-major chorus.

**New building blocks:**

1. **Progressions:**
   - **Verse loop:** i │ ♭VII/i │ v7 │ IV, Dorian (major IV, minor v), with ♭VII over the tonic
     bass in bar 2. It is close to TF's verse family but adds the slash chord and the v7.
   - **Chorus loop:** III(add9) │ VI⁶ │ ♭II │ VII7sus4→VII7, i.e. a I–IV⁶–♭VII–V7 cadence in the
     relative major. The generator has no loop yet that changes tonal centre between sections.
   - **Joins:** IV → III into the chorus, VII7 → i out of it.
   - **Substitutions it demonstrates:** ♭VII over the tonic pedal; the first-inversion VI⁶ that
     keeps the tonic note in the bass; the Neapolitan-coloured ♭II; the 7sus4 → 7 on the
     dominant.
2. **Form blocks:**
   - an **F-pedal intro block**: 2 bars of i │ ♭VII/i, 2 bars of a two-note riff alone, which
     returns as a mid-song reprise;
   - a **2-bar bass drop** before the verse loop starts;
   - a sparse sung **verse** (12 bars, only after an intro block), and a 16-bar **synth-lead
     section** on the same loop before every chorus, which replaces the second verse;
   - a 16-bar chorus = the chorus loop ×4 with the voice in long notes;
   - a **second half that repeats the first** (reprise, verse, synth lead, chorus) instead of a
     bridge, and a **fade on the chorus loop**;
   - for an instrumental or 12″ mode: a 16-bar **bass-less break on the verse chords**.
3. **A bass style:** root 8ths ("disco 8ths") **accented on the off-beats**, legato, dark (nothing
   much above 300 Hz), octave-layered, with **octave lifts at fixed 8ths in the pattern** (beats 2–3 of the loop's
   first bar, the last beat before a chord change) and no anticipations. It has the off-beat feel
   of the Italo octave bass but **not** its root–octave alternation; if that alternation is an
   Italo trait, it has to come from the other references.
4. **Drum traits:**
   - a **static machine groove**: kick on every beat, clap-plus-snare on 2 and 4, quiet 8th hats,
     no fills and no crashes for a whole song;
   - an **off-beat open hat added in the chorus only**;
   - a deep pitch-swept kick (≈100 → 36 Hz) and a multi-burst clap layered on a snare;
   - toms belong to edits and fills only, not to the groove.
5. **Arrangement:** pads voiced as **triads stacked in three octaves** (C4–A♭6); a **16th-note
   arpeggio** of chord tones through the verse loop; a low, reverbed **lead synth that answers
   the voice** in its own 16-bar sections; long vocal notes in the chorus.
6. **Tempo and key:** a genre range around 110 BPM, below the synthpop 116–121; minor keys with a
   tonic pedal and a Dorian IV, and a chorus that may move to the relative major.
