## 3. Tonal centre: F minor, Dorian, with a relative-major chorus

The song is in **F minor**:

- The bass sits on F 40–43% of the time, and every intro and pedal block is an F pedal.
- The whole-song key fit gives F minor (r 0.66 original, 0.73 maxi), well clear of the
  next candidate (F major 0.46, E♭ major 0.48).
- The verse loop starts on Fm and every return to the verse lands on it (E♭ → Fm, VII → i).

The verse is **Dorian**: its IV is B♭ **major**, with D natural held across three octaves in the
pad (D4, D5, D6). Its v is Cm7, minor, with no E natural. The only clear E natural is the bass's
passing E1 on beat 4 of bar 4, a leading tone into the drop.

The chorus is the question. Its loop is A♭ │ D♭/F │ G♭ │ E♭7sus4 → E♭, and the E♭ chord carries a
D♭ (its 7th). Heard in A♭ major that is **I │ IV⁶ │ ♭VII │ V7sus4 → V**, a dominant cadence back
into A♭ every 4 bars: the chorus tonicises the relative major. In F minor the same chords are
III │ VI⁶ │ ♭II │ VII. The G♭ is the one chord outside F Aeolian or Dorian: a Neapolitan ♭II in F
minor, or a borrowed ♭VII in A♭. The record never cadences in A♭ for good; the last chorus fades on
the loop, and every chorus–verse join goes E♭ → Fm.

**Verdict: F minor (Dorian verse), with the chorus moving to the relative major for its 16 bars.**
It is the reverse of You Came, which kept its tonic back; here the tonic is a 40% pedal, and the
chorus is where it lets go. The automatic "G♯ major" and "A♯ minor" readings in the written sources
(§8) come from the untuned A♭ chorus and from the 42-cent flat tuning.

## 5. The bass

**Not the classic octave bass.** There is no 8th-by-8th alternation between the root and its
octave. The bass plays **legato root 8ths** ("disco 8ths": a note on every beat and every "&", none
on the "e" or "a"), accented on the off-beats, and moves up the octave for 2–6 8ths at a time in
fixed places.

How this was checked: for every 8th, the level at the root's fundamental was compared with the
level at its octave. In an octave bass the two swap every 8th. Here they stay level (within 3 dB,
H2 − H1 between −3 and +2 dB on every 8th of the bar, averaged over the verse); the octave stretches
show up as the fundamental dropping 15–30 dB for a few 8ths, then coming back.

- **Every 8th is struck, the off-beats harder.** The average 8th rises to its peak in 30–40 ms
  and decays about 13 dB before the next one, so the line pumps without gaps. In the bass stem the
  "&" notes attack 2–6 dB harder than the on-beat ones in the 3rd–5th harmonics, and in the full
  mix the kick covers the on-beat attacks, so what you hear is a bass that **hits on the
  off-beats** against the kick on the beats. By ear, the user hears the off-beats as the sequenced
  bass. It is the off-beat feel of the Italo octave bass, made with one pitch instead of two.
- **The octave lifts are part of the pattern, not fills**, and they repeat exactly wherever a
  section repeats:
  - in the sung verses, the first F bar of every loop goes up the octave for 2–4 8ths around
    beats 2–3 (bars 23, 27, 31, 35 and their repeats); the lead-synth sections don't lift there;
  - the second F bar often ends with 2–3 8ths up, leading into the C;
  - the C bar occasionally lifts around beats 3–4 (C3 in bars 29 and 61);
  - in the first loop of each chorus, the F bar ends up the octave and carries into the G♭ bar,
    which plays G♭2 for its first 3–4 8ths and drops to G♭1 (bars 40–41, 72–73, 130–131).
- **No anticipation, no 16ths.** Every root change lands on beat 1, and nothing falls on the "e"
  or "a" (the automatic "B1" passing notes in some C bars are a pitch-tracker artefact: there is no
  energy a semitone below the root there).
- **Register: E♭1–G♭2** (39–92 Hz), with C3 as an occasional top. It is lower than You Came's
  G♯1–F♯2 because the chorus reaches E♭1. The verse lives on F1, C2 and B♭1 (43–65 Hz).
- **Timbre:** very dark. The 2nd harmonic is level with the fundamental (+1 to −7 dB): an octave
  layer, as in You Came. The 3rd is 11–20 dB down, and nothing above the 4th–6th harmonic is within
  22 dB of the fundamental, so the content stops at 150–390 Hz.
- **Where it plays:** everywhere except the two 2-bar drops (bars 5–6, 95–96) and the
  maxi's 16-bar break. In the intro it enters with the drums on the first beat, and in bar
  4 it walks F–F–F–E1 into the drop.
- **The maxi** carries the same bass part. Its bass stem reads the octave (F2, C3) far more
  often, for whole half-bars (maxi 0:09–0:13 is almost all F2), but by ear the two stems sound the

## 9. What The Night adds to the generator

**Where it sits.**

| Hats     | beat, &, a                        | quiet 8ths; an open off-beat hat only in the chorus                              |

Against the three synthpop references it is slower (110 against 117–120), more static (one drum bar for the whole record), and harmonically richer in the chorus. It shares the
four-on-the-floor kick, the root-8th bass and the 4-bar loop logic. What reads as "Italo" in it is
less any single device than the combination: a machine groove that never changes, pads in stacked
octaves, an echoed lead synth, a plucked chorus countervoice, a minor verse over a tonic pedal
and a relative-major chorus.

**New building blocks:**

1. **Progressions:**
   - **Verse loop:** i │ ♭VII/i │ v7 │ IV, Dorian (major IV, minor v), with ♭VII over the tonic
     bass in bar 2. It is close to TF's verse family but adds the slash chord and the v7.
   - **Chorus loop:** III(add9) │ VI⁶ │ ♭II │ VII7sus4→VII7, i.e. a I–IV⁶–♭VII–V7 cadence in the
     relative major. The generator has no loop yet that changes tonal centre between sections.
   - **Joins:** IV → III into the chorus, VII7 → i out of it.
   - **Substitutions it demonstrates:** ♭VII over the tonic pedal; the first-inversion VI⁶ that
     keeps the tonic note in the bass; the Neapolitan-coloured ♭II; the 7sus4 → 7 on the
     dominant.
2. **Form blocks:**
   - an **F-pedal intro block**: 2 bars of i │ ♭VII/i, 2 bars of a two-note riff alone, which
     returns as a mid-song reprise;
   - a **2-bar bass drop** before the verse loop starts;
   - a 12-bar **lead-synth section** on the verse loop after each intro block, before the first
     voice, and a 16-bar sung **verse** before every chorus;
   - a 16-bar chorus = the chorus loop ×4 with the voice in long notes;
   - a **second half that repeats the first** (reprise, lead synth, verse, chorus) instead of a
     bridge, and a **fade on the chorus loop**;
   - for a 12″/maxi mode (from the maxi, §2): start straight on the loop without the intro;
     replay the lead-synth and verse sections as backing only; add a 16-bar **bass-less break on
     the verse chords**, a longer pedal block and a no-bass fill with toms before the last chorus
     (joined cleanly: the maxi's tape splice is not reproduced);
     keep the voice for one chorus.
3. **A bass style:** root 8ths ("disco 8ths") **accented on the off-beats**, legato, dark (nothing
   much above 300 Hz), octave-layered, with **octave lifts at fixed 8ths in the pattern** (beats 2–3 of the loop's
   first bar, the last beat before a chord change) and no anticipations. It has the off-beat feel
   of the Italo octave bass but **not** its root–octave alternation; if that alternation is an
   Italo trait, it has to come from the other references.
4. **Drum traits:**
   - a **static machine groove**: kick on every beat, clap-plus-snare on 2 and 4, quiet 8th hats,
     no fills and no crashes for a whole song;
   - an **off-beat open hat added in the chorus only**;
   - a deep pitch-swept kick (≈100 → 36 Hz) and a multi-burst clap layered on a snare;
   - toms only as single hits in the maxi's one fill, never in the groove.
5. **Arrangement:** pads voiced as **triads stacked in three octaves** (C4–A♭6); a soft, echoed
   **lead synth** that opens each half before the voice; a **plucked countervoice** on the chorus
   8ths; a low, wet verse voice and long vocal notes in the chorus. The patches are in §10.
6. **Tempo and key:** a genre range around 110 BPM, below the synthpop 116–121; minor keys with a
   tonic pedal and a Dorian IV, and a chorus that may move to the relative major.

## 10. Synth parts for the generator

The Italo genre on main gives The Night a "lead synth" on the `lead` track: the voice-like mono
synth (two detuned saws, portamento, a 5.2 Hz vibrato, a filter sweep) playing an F3–C4 line in
16-bar sections before every chorus, after the earlier reading of §2. By ear, its intro lead
"doesn't have the correct timbre at all", and the chorus lacks its signature countervoice. On the
record:

- the F3–C4 line before each chorus is **the sung verse**, not a synth (clips 12a–12d);
- **the lead synth** is a soft, nearly pure tone in E♭4–C5, in 12-bar sections after each intro
  block (§10.1);
- **the chorus countervoice** is a plucked line on the 8ths, in the voice's register, on
  the chord tones (§10.2).

Both parts were measured on the stems and checked by ear with A/B clips (the ear-check table
below). Timings are at 110.26 BPM: an 8th is 272 ms, a dotted 8th 408 ms.

### 10.1 The lead synth

**Where:** bars 11–22 and 101–112, the 12 bars after each intro block, over the verse loop; by ear
its first notes come in over the loop from bar 7 (0:13.8, clip 18). The maxi keeps four fragments
(maxi 0:13, 0:31, 2:19, 4:33). It never plays under the voice.

bars with rests between them (4 bars of phrases, 2 bars rest, 6 more); it sounds about 40% of the

**Patch for the existing voices**, a 1:1 FM tone whose index falls from a bright strike to a
nearly pure sustain (at the sustained index of about 0.6, the 2nd harmonic sits 9 dB under the
fundamental and the 3rd about 25 dB under, as measured):

```ts
new NativeFMSynth({
  harmonicity: 1,
  // The modulator runs at -10 dB, so the index heard is 0.32 × this: about 2.4 at the strike,
  // held at about 0.6 by the modulation envelope's sustain.
  modulationIndex: 7.5,
  oscillator: { type: "sine" },
  modulation: { type: "sine" },
  envelope: { attack: 0.004, decay: 0.15, sustain: 0.35, release: 0.1 },
  modulationEnvelope: { attack: 0.002, decay: 0.08, sustain: 0.25, release: 0.1 },
});
```

- **Vibrato:** the voices have none of their own. Put it after the synth, as the `lead` track does,
  with `new Tone.Vibrato({ frequency: 3, depth: 0.22 })`: with Tone's default 5 ms maximum delay,
  depth 0.22 at 3 Hz swings the pitch about ±18 cents.
- **Echo:** the shared delay bus is already a dotted 8th (`"8n."`, feedback 0.32), but it is
  high-passed at 600 Hz, which removes most of a tone whose fundamental is 310–520 Hz. Send about
  0.4 to a dotted-8th delay without that high-pass (feedback about 0.25), or lower the bus's
  high-pass for this track.
- **Reverb:** the shared reverb (3.2 s) is about three times longer than the record's; keep the send
  low (about 0.15).
- **Range:** E♭4–C5 (MIDI 63–72), with a note's room either side. The composer's
  `LEAD_SYNTH_RANGE` (52–65, E3–F4) is the verse voice's register instead.
- **Track:** a voice of its own rather than the `lead` track: the `lead` track plays the voice
  (§10.3), and The Night needs both, though never at once.

**Role:** an instrumental hook that opens each half, after the pedal intro (and after the reprise),
for 12 bars, before the first verse; sparse phrases with rests, never under the voice. In the
maxi, a few fragments over the backing-only replays.

### 10.2 The chorus countervoice

**Where:** every bar of every chorus (the three in the original, the maxi's three), and nowhere
else: by ear it is absent from the verse loop, the sung verse and the maxi's break (clips 18, 19,
15). It enters on the chorus's first bar and stops with it.

**What it plays:** the line comes from the user's MIDI transcription
(`inspiration/Valerie Dore/the night chorus countervoice.mid`, local only, written in C at 90 BPM).
Transposed **up 8 semitones**, with its bar 1 on the chorus's A♭ bar, it fits the record far
better than any other placement: the record's struck-note salience at its notes is 3.68, against
2.90 an octave higher, 1.97 an octave lower and at most 2.61 with the bars rotated (maxi 33–48).
By ear the user hears it as right (clips 24a, 24b). In A♭, one 4-bar loop, repeated through the
whole chorus:

| -------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A♭(add9) | –   | E♭4 | E♭5 | D♭5 | C5  | B♭4 | A♭4 | A♭4 |
| D♭/F     | –   | E♭4 | C5  | D♭5 | –   | A♭4 | C5  | B♭4 |

- **Every bar starts the same way:** nothing on beat 1, a low E♭4 on its "&" (the only note held
  for two 16ths), then a run from beat 2 on the 8ths.
- **The runs step through the A♭ major scale,** mostly downwards from the top of the range: a
  full descent E♭5 to A♭4 in the A♭ bar, D♭5 to A♭4 in the G♭ bar. The D♭/F and E♭ bars turn

  the voice's register, as heard (clips 13a, 13b).
- The same loop plays in every chorus. The note E♭ (the chorus's dominant) on the "&" of 1 of

My own readings from the stems were wrong (clips 20a–20f and 23: "completely off", "still off"),
because the pads, the open hat on the same off-beats and the reverb of earlier notes all leak into
them. Only the first bar of my first reading (E♭5 D♭5 … B♭4 A♭4 A♭4) matched.

**Its sound,** found by ear in four rounds against the maxi's voiceless chorus (maxi 1:10.0),
each candidate level-matched and played alone after the record:

1. Six different sounds (clips 20a–20f: the saw pluck then on main, an FM mallet, an FM electric
   piano, a square pluck, string pizzicato, a soft pluck with a tail): the saw pluck (20a) was the
   closest, but the record's sound is **less round, brighter, and its notes ring longer**.
2. Brighter and longer variants of the saw pluck (clips 21a–21e): **an open saw with long notes and

3. The two alone and layered (clips 22a–22d): **the two layered at equal loudness (22c)** was the

4. On the user's MIDI line (clips 24a, 24b): the 12 ms attack (24a) beat the 2 ms one, and the
   layer is "close, keep it".

What the stems measure agrees with a bright, quickly decaying pluck: the fundamental falls about
15 dB in the first 70 ms of a note, the onset is centred (left and right within about 1 dB), and
the "other" stem around it is wide (side 6 dB under mid, pads included). The reverb can't be
measured under the pads; by ear it is heavy.

**Patch: two layers, equal loudness, on the `counter` track.**

```ts

new NativeMonoSynth({
  oscillator: { type: "sawtooth" },
  filter: { type: "lowpass", rolloff: -12, Q: 0.8 },

  envelope: { attack: 0.012, decay: 1.4, sustain: 0.05, release: 0.35 },
  filterEnvelope: {
    attack: 0.002,
    decay: 0.12,
    sustain: 0.3,
    release: 0.15,
    baseFrequency: 5000,
    octaves: 0.5,
  },
});
// Layer 2, TF's electric piano, the `counter` patch already in instruments.ts, with the same
// softer attack: a 1:1 FM strike (index about 0.5 heard) on a carrier with the e-piano partials.
new NativeFMSynth({
  harmonicity: 1,
  modulationIndex: 1.6,
  oscillator: { type: "sine", partials: [1, 0.56, 0.28, 0.14, 0.08, 0.056, 0.018, 0.016] },
  modulation: { type: "sine" },
  envelope: { attack: 0.012, decay: 7.3, sustain: 0.15, release: 0.35 },
  modulationEnvelope: { attack: 0.002, decay: 0.9, sustain: 0, release: 0.2 },
});
```

- **Balance:** the two at equal loudness (the clips matched their RMS on the same line); set the
  volumes by meter, not by the option values, since the voices' gain staging differs.
- **Reverb:** heavy. The clips used a 2.8 s tail about 4 dB under the dry signal, so a send of
  about 0.6–0.7 to the shared 3.2 s reverb. No delay.

**Role:** the chorus's signature second line, following the chords bar by bar under and between
the long vocal notes, in the voice's register. In the generator it should play in every chorus of
a song in The Night's style (it isn't a chance draw on the record), and never outside the chorus.
For a generated line in the same shape: rest on beat 1; the chorus key's 5th, low, on its "&";
then 8ths from beat 2 stepping down the scale from about an octave above that 5th, landing on
chord tones, with one or two rests a bar. The generator's current line (`pluckedCountervoice`:
chord tones from the top down, on the off-beats and a few beats) has the range and the 8ths but
not the low pickup or the scale runs.

### 10.3 Anything else the generator lacks

- **There is no 16th arpeggio.** The earlier reading of a 16th arpeggio through the verse loop and

  lead synth (clip 18) and the break has none (clip 15). The Night's `verseArp` weight in the
  Italo genre comes from that reading and should go.
- **The verse voice is low, wet and wide.** F3–C4 (the Italo genre's verse range is G3–G4), dense
  (it sings 60–95% of the time), with a slow vibrato (3.7 Hz, ±15–20 cents, the same in both
  channels) and a long, wide reverb: side only 3–6 dB under mid where the lead synth is mono, a
  45 ms offset between the channels, tails with RT60 about 1.5–2 s after the last note of a
  verse. By ear, a resynthesis with that space (clip 12a) matched better than a dry one (12d).
- **The intro riff is a third patch.** The two-note F4–E♭4 figure in bars 3–6 is brighter than
  the lead synth and sits in the "other" stem: h2 −9 dB, h3 −14, h4 −17, h5 −16, h6–h8 −18 to
