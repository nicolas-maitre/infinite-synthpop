## 2. Tuning, tempo and tonal centre

- The remaster runs as long as the original 12″: 5:40 against 5:38.

So the instruments were tuned off concert pitch. That fits a studio where everything was tuned to
one synth rather than to a fork.

The notes here use the **lower names**: E at +47 cents, not F at −53. That gives the simpler
spelling, the C major / A minor pair and A major. With the upper names the song would be in the
D♭ / B♭ minor pair, moving to B♭ major. The automatic pass leans the same way: it calls the record
C major. For the generator the offset doesn't matter. It picks the nearest named key (§9).

### 2.3 Tonal centre: A, from the minor side to the major

The song has two harmonic areas, and both are anchored on **A**:

- **The verse loop** is Fmaj7 – Gsus4 │ Em7 – Am7. In C major that is IV–V–iii–vi, the progression
  Japanese pop calls the "royal road" (_ōdō shinkō_). It never cadences in C: G goes to Em, not to
  C.
  - Each two-bar cycle **lands on Am** and holds it for a bar and a half.
  - A synth on top holds C and G the whole time (§7), so the C-major colour never goes away.
  - The verse alone fits no key well: C major 0.44, D minor 0.44, F major 0.42, A minor 0.41.
    That is the ambiguity the royal road is known for.
- **The chorus** is in **A major**, and clearly so (r 0.74). Its chords are A, Em7, Dmaj7, F♯m7,
  G and E, with C♯ in the voice and the pads. The G♮ and the minor v (Em7) give it a Mixolydian
  edge.

What joins them is the **A**:

- **Into the chorus**, the Am that ends the verse becomes A major at the chorus downbeat (bar 39 →
  40, 1:18 → 1:20). Nothing pivots. The root stays, and the third rises.
- **Back into the verse**, the chorus ends on E (V of A) and goes straight to the verse's F (bar
  55 → 56, 1:50 → 1:52). That is V → ♭VI, A minor's deceptive cadence.

So the verse sits in A minor's relative-major world, the chorus in its parallel major. Heard from
A, the song's harmonic event is a **minor-to-major lift at every chorus**.

The whole-song fit splits between A minor (0.46) and A major (0.40) for the same reason. The
automatic pass's "C major" (r 0.67) is the verse and the instrumentals outvoting the choruses.

**TR-909** at short decay best, or an analog drum synth. A Roland TR-707 (1985) is possible for
the hats and snare. **Confidence: low.**

## 9. What Faces adds to the generator

**Where it sits.** Next to the synthpop references it keeps the essentials:

- the tempo (120, against 117–120);
- a four-on-the-floor kick and a 2-and-4 snare;
- sections in multiples of 8 bars;
- an instrumental opening over the loop.

It differs on five axes, and each of them is a building block the Italo genre needs:

| Axis    | Synthpop (TF, DS, You Came)                                     | Faces                                                                                                          |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Harmony | one key per song; minor (TF, DS) or major (You Came)            | **two modes on one root**: the minor-side royal-road loop, the **parallel-major chorus**, and back by V → ♭VI  |
| Loop    | i–III–VII–iv/IV, or You Came's IV–V                             | **IV△7–Vsus4–iii7–vi7** (royal road) under a 1-and-5 pedal                                                     |
| Bass    | root 8ths, legato (You Came) or the shared 16th figure (TF, DS) | **a staccato 2-bar figure, on-beat then off-beat**, next root on **4&**; a **second, dark bass** in the chorus |
| Hats    | dense 16ths (TF, DS) or beat-&-a (You Came)                     | **8ths with an open 3a** in the verse, a **galloping 16th cell** in the chorus                                 |
| Form    | album and 12″ forms with a bridge                               | a **DJ-oriented 12″**: a 24-bar build before the voice, long instrumentals, a 16-bar breakdown, a fade         |

**New building blocks:**

1. **Progressions:**
   - **The royal-road loop:** IV△7 – Vsus4 │ iii7 – vi7 in the relative major, i.e. VI△7 –
     ♭VIIsus4 │ v7 – i7 from the minor tonic. G, Gsus4 and G11 are interchangeable on the V.
     Two chords a bar, with the vi pushed in on 2& and the next bar's root on 4&.
   - **A top pedal** on degrees 1 and 5 of the relative major (C and G here) held through the
     whole loop.
   - **An intro variant** that stays on iii for the second bar and only completes the loop when
     the snare enters.
   - **The major chorus** on the minor loop's tonic: I │ v7–IV△7, three times, then I–vi7 │
     ♭VII–V7sus4. Its second half doubles the turnaround: I │ v7–IV△7 │ I │ v7–IV△7 │ I–vi7 │
     ♭VII–V7sus4 │ I–vi7 │ ♭VII–V7sus4. The dominant never has its leading tone: the minor v7 in
     the body, the sus4 in the turnaround.
   - **The links:** vi (minor) → I (major) on the same root into the chorus. V → ♭VI back into
     the verse (A minor's deceptive cadence).
   - A chromatic bass walk I–(♯7)–♭7–6 (A–G♯–G–F♯) as a variation of I–vi.
2. **Bass styles:**
   - **On-beat / off-beat staccato:** a two-bar figure on the chord roots. Short notes on beats
     1, 2, 3 and 4 in the first bar; on 1&, 2& and 3& in the second. Each bar ends legato into
     the next root on 4&. Octave hops only at the bar line (the last beat up an octave, the pickup
     in that octave).
     - The sound is a bright, plucky saw with a sub-octave, the octave partial louder than the
       fundamental.
     - Range E1–A1, with E2 and G2 hops.
   - **A sustained chorus bass:** whole and half notes on the root, dark (H2 ≈ H1, H3 about
     −20 dB), D1–A1.
   - **A bass rule per section:** drop the bass from the first verse and the first chorus bar,
     bring it back for the second verse.
   - This song does **not** supply the alternating octave bass the genre is known for. If The Night
     or Get Closer has it, it comes from them.
3. **Form blocks** (a 12″ mode for Italo):
   - an intro in three 4- and 16-bar steps: bass and kick → + stabs and quarter hats → full groove;
   - the instrumental loop as the verse's twin (8 bars after a chorus, 24 before the last
     choruses), with a synth lead in the long one;
   - **a 16-bar breakdown on the chorus turnaround, without bass or voice**, with an off-beat
     arpeggio;
   - an outro of the chorus's second half, faded;
   - 16-bar verses and 16-bar choruses (8 + 8).
4. **Drum traits:**
   - hats by section: none → quarters → 8ths with an open 3a → the **x x x . │ x . x x** 16th cell
     in choruses and the breakdown;
   - a kick on 3& in every 4th chorus bar;
   - a **3&–4–4& snare pickup every 8 bars**, no tom fills;
   - a **clave on 3a and 4& (and 1&)** in the breakdown only, plus a rare quiet tom;
   - a deep, pitch-swept short kick (909-like);
   - the kick never stops.
5. **Arrangement roles:**
   - **gated 8th-note chord stabs** as the rhythmic layer under everything except the breakdown;
   - a held two-note pedal on top in the verse world;
   - sustained pads only in the major chorus;
   - an off-beat 16th arpeggio for the breakdown;
   - a lead synth that plays in the long instrumental;
   - a low, narrow verse voice (C4–E4) and a chorus a fifth higher (around A4).
6. **Key and tuning:** the key choice gains the **two-mode song** (a minor-side verse, a major
   chorus on the same root). Tempo stays at 120. The quarter-tone tuning is a property of this
   record, not of the genre; the generator should ignore it.

**Generic Italo versus this song.** Generic Italo, and worth making defaults for the genre:

- 120 BPM;
- the kick on every beat for the whole record;
- gated 8th stabs;
- a bright, plucky sequenced bass;
- 16th hats in the chorus;
- a 12″ form with long instrumentals and a breakdown;
- a minor-side verse and a brighter chorus.

Specific to Faces, and better kept as choices with low weight:

- the royal-road loop with its C/G pedal;
- the same-root lift from vi to I;
- the dark second bass;
- verse 1 without bass;
- the galloping hat cell (possibly generic too; the Valerie Dore analyses will tell);
- the off-concert tuning.

**Against Valerie Dore (to confirm with the other two analyses).** The user chose Clio as the more
Italo-disco-adjacent corner of the genre. On these measurements that means a bright, fully
synthetic, DJ-shaped record, with these traits to compare against The Night and Get Closer:

- **Mode:** a minor-side verse lifting to a major chorus on the same root. Check whether Valerie
  Dore stays minor throughout.
- **Bass:** a staccato on-beat/off-beat figure on the root, not alternating octaves. If the
  Valerie Dore songs use the alternating octave bass, that becomes the genre's other bass style,
  and Faces' pluck the contrast.
- **Drums:** the kick never stops, there are no claps and almost no toms, a clave appears only
  in the breakdown, and the hats change by section. Check the Valerie Dore records for claps,
  toms, percussion and fills.
- **Form:** a long build before the voice, a long instrumental with a lead synth, a breakdown, no
  vocoder. Check them for vocoder or vocal-sample breaks.
- **Timbre:** stabs and pedal in the verses, pads in the chorus, an arpeggio in the breakdown.
