# DJ mode

Issues #4 (DJ mode) and #5 (DJ tricks). Today a song plays to its end and the next one follows
after a two-bar handoff. In DJ mode, each song plays long enough to give its gist, then mixes
into the next one like a DJ set: no gap, no tempo jump.

## Decisions (from the issues)

- A set is live only: no set link, no set seed. Each song keeps its own seed, so Share and Save
  work on whatever plays.
- A song mixes out at a section line, no earlier than after its first verse–chorus pass (about
  2 minutes). The odds rise section by section, and after the second chorus is the likeliest.
  Some songs play to their end, with the next one mixed in over the outro.
- Tempo: the next song's own tempo is within ±6 % of the current one. Glides run at about 1 BPM
  per 16 bars. Bigger distances are covered over several songs, as the set drifts.
- Two transition styles, crossfade and morph, plus auto, which picks one per transition (morph
  when the next song is close, crossfade when it's far).
- The trick in use is shown on screen ("Mixing → Get Closer").
- Tricks are automatic for now. Each trick is its own action that the automatic DJ chooses from,
  so a manual DJ panel can call the same entry point later.
- No special treatment for lite mode.
- First tricks: key matching (harmonic mixing, pitch to match), the mashup overlap, echo out and
  the acapella bridge.

## Decision made here

- **DJ mode plays render ahead.** Render-ahead playback (`src/ahead.ts`) already renders a
  transition as one piece with a deck (its own `Instruments`) per song. Live playback has one set
  of instruments, and a second deck there would double the live CPU load, which is what render
  ahead exists to avoid. With DJ on, playback renders ahead whatever the Playback setting says.
  Where the browser can't render ahead (no AudioWorklet), the DJ switch is disabled.

## How a crossfade plays

Both songs share the outgoing song's tempo `b1` during the overlap. The incoming song is
composed at its own tempo `b2` (±6 % of `b1`) and a key that suits the outgoing song's.

Let `S` be the swap bar: a section line in the outgoing song where its last phrase ends. The
incoming song's bar 0 starts at `S`, and a **mix-in intro** of 16 bars plays before it:

| Bars (incoming) | Outgoing song            | Incoming song                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| −16 … −9        | everything               | its groove without the kick, fading in (hats, snare, …) |
| −8 … −1         | everything but the drums | its full groove                                         |
| 0 …             | its tails ring out       | the song from its first bar, at `b1`, gliding to `b2`   |

- The mix-in intro's groove is the incoming song's first full-drum bars (the start of its first
  verse or chorus), looped 4 bars at a time, so it has no fills.
- At the swap, bass and harmony change hands in one bar line: the outgoing bass, chords, riffs
  and voice stop at the end of their phrase, and the incoming song's own arrangement takes over.
  Two harmonies never play over each other. The voice handoff is where the echo out and the
  acapella bridge come in later.
- If the incoming song's first bars have no drums (a beatless intro), the mix-in groove keeps
  going until its own drums come in, for at most 16 bars, so the beat never drops.
- After the swap, the incoming song glides from `b1` to `b2` at 1 BPM per 16 bars.

The render-ahead player needs two things it doesn't have yet:

1. **A tempo map per deck.** A deck's bar `n` starts at `origin + n × bar length` today. With
   glides (and the mix-in intro's negative bars), a deck has a clock: the tempo per bar and the
   time each bar starts. Bars change tempo one bar at a time, in steps of about 0.06 BPM, which
   is far below what anyone hears.
2. **What a deck plays per bar.** Today a deck plays `arrangeBar(song, bar)`. A DJ deck plays the
   mix-in groove for negative bars and filters its bars around the swap (no kick, no drums, no
   harmony), with velocities scaled for the fade-in.

The plan for a song (its mix-out point, the next song, the swap bar) is worked out once per song
and kept with it, so a re-render (a fader change, a settings change) mid-transition plans the same
transition again.

## Choosing the next song

- **Style:** the next song is drawn from the settings as today (genre, blend or auto).
- **Tempo:** its own draw, clamped to ±6 % of the outgoing tempo, on the half-BPM grid. A fixed
  Tempo setting keeps every song at that tempo, and nothing glides.
- **Key:** harmonic mixing on the Camelot wheel. If its own key is a safe move from the outgoing
  key (the same key, a fifth up or down, the relative major or minor), it stays. Otherwise one of
  the safe moves in the song's own mode is drawn. A fixed Key setting keeps every song in that
  key. The outgoing key is the key of the section that ends at the swap (a lifted ending counts).

## Phases

1. **Clocks and deck plans in render ahead.** `Deck` gets a tempo map and an optional per-bar
   arrangement. Nothing sounds different yet. Unit tests for the tempo map (bar times, the bar at
   a time, glides).
2. **Crossfade DJ.** The DJ switch, the mix-out point, the next song's tempo and key, the mix-in
   intro and swap, the glide, and the "Mixing → Title" line. Unit tests for the pure parts (mix-out
   point, tempo and key choice, the deck plans). A transition rendered offline for an ear check.
3. **Morph and auto.** The morph style (the blend point, tempo and mix gliding toward the next
   song, which enters at a breakdown or build) and auto per transition.
4. **First tricks (#5).** Pitch to match (transposing the incoming song for the overlap, then
   modulating back), echo out on the outgoing voice, the acapella bridge, and mashup variants of
   the handoff (riff carry-over, drums-only bridge).
