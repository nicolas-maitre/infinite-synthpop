# Euro-disco genre

The third genre, built from three Sandra records, all produced by Michael Cretu and co-written by
Hubert Kemmler:

- "Innocent Love" (1986, `docs/references/sandra-innocent-love.md`): 115.2 BPM, E♭ minor.
- "Heaven Can Wait" (1988, `sandra-heaven-can-wait.md`): 117.9 BPM (verses) and 119.1 (choruses),
  E♭ minor, tuned 29 cents sharp.
- "Crazy Juliet" (1988, `sandra-crazy-juliet.md`): 123.0 BPM, C♯ (a Mixolydian verse, and a chorus
  on the minor v that never sounds the tonic).

The two E♭-minor records share the triangle's bottom edge, Innocent Love on the left (the legacy
`style` 0) and Heaven Can Wait on the right (1). Crazy Juliet, the fastest and the only major one,
sits at the top.

The name fits loosely: none of the three is four-on-the-floor. It is the Cretu/Sandra sound of
1986–88, a backbeat pop kit under minor-key synthpop harmony.

## What the three share (the genre's defaults)

- **A backbeat kick, not four-on-the-floor.** The kick is on 1 and 3, never on 2 or 4, with
  syncopated extra hits that change bar by bar in 2- or 4-bar cycles. The chorus kick is busier
  than the verse kick.
- **The snare on 2 and 4**, with a long, ungated tail. No claps, and toms only in Crazy Juliet's
  intro.
- **Hats that come and go between sections**: sparse or none in the verses, nearly every 16th in
  Heaven Can Wait's and Crazy Juliet's choruses, never the "e" right after a snare.
- **Syncopated riff basses with rests**, a different figure in the verse and the chorus. Beats 2 and
  4 are usually left to the snare. They are octave-layered and dark (harmonics within 22 dB only up
  to 300–420 Hz) and very low: D♯1–A♭2.
- **Pre-choruses, and choruses back to back.** Choruses are 10 or 14 bars, not 8 or 16. The second
  half of each song is mostly choruses, one of them instrumental, and it ends on a chorus to fade.
- **Stops instead of fills.** Innocent Love and Crazy Juliet stop the band before the chorus.
  Heaven Can Wait and Crazy Juliet mark phrase ends with short snare pickups.
- **Harmony next to synthpop's.** The VI–VII climb and i–VII movement, the Dorian IV in one section
  and the Aeolian iv in another, and the harmonic-minor V (often V sus4 → V) before the chorus or
  at its end. The Different Story, also a Cretu/Kemmler record, is its nearest synthpop
  neighbour.
- **Tempo 115–123.**
