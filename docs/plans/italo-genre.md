# Italo genre

The second genre (issue #1), built from three measured references:

- Valerie Dore, "The Night" (`docs/italo/the-night.md`): 110.3 BPM, F minor.
- Valerie Dore, "Get Closer" (`docs/italo/get-closer.md`): 111.8 BPM, E minor. Merged once its ear
  check is answered.
- Clio, "Faces" (`docs/italo/faces.md`): 120.1 BPM, A minor with an A major chorus.

The two Valerie Dore records are near-twins (same writers, producers and label). Faces is the
brighter, DJ-shaped corner. In the style triangle, The Night and Get Closer share the bottom edge,
and Faces sits at the top.

## What the three share (the genre's defaults)

- A machine groove: the kick on every beat for the whole record and the snare on 2 and 4. No tom
  grooves and few fills.
- Hats in 8ths, not 16ths. The chorus opens them up: an open off-beat hat (The Night) or a
  galloping 16th cell (Faces).
- A plain bass on the chord roots, with no synthpop 16th figure.
- Minor verses, a brighter chorus, and 16-bar blocks.
- Long instrumental sections over the verse loop, with a 16th line or arpeggio on top.
- A breakdown without bass, where the kick keeps going.
- Every record ends on a fade.

**No octave bass.** None of the three uses the alternating root–octave bass that Italo is known
for: The Night and Get Closer play root 8ths, and Faces plays a staccato on-beat/off-beat figure.
The genre gets the bass styles that were actually measured. An octave bass can come later from a
reference that has one.
