# Theory-based chord progressions

**Goal:** generated songs write their own progressions from _why_ the references' progressions
work (their mode colour, bass motion, cadences, contrast between sections), instead of picking a
measured loop and lightly varying it. Today's generator stays available, unchanged, as
**classic progressions**.

**Status:** phase 1 (plumbing) done; theory mode still plays classic's chords. Forms (section order and lengths) get the same treatment in a later phase
(§8).

## 1. The problem, measured

400 songs per genre, a random style point per song (the "auto" style), compared with the banks and
the references (2026-11-17):

|                                              | Synthpop | Italo | Euro |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----- | ---- |
| Distinct verse progressions                  | 41       | 25    | 6    |
| Verses exactly a bank entry                  | 77%      | 81%   | 100% |
| Verses exactly a reference record's verse    | 9%       | 50%   | 71%  |
| Distinct chorus progressions                 | 32       | 10    | 28   |
| Choruses exactly a reference record's chorus | 34%      | 55%   | 65%  |
| Distinct progressions within one song        | 5.6      | 3.1   | 6.3  |

Why:

- **The bank is a menu.** A song picks one whole entry. Measured entries weigh 2, generic ones
  0.5, so near a corner of the triangle the record's own loop usually wins.
- **Substitutions barely move it.** They touch 30% of chords, never bar 1, the first match only,
  from 11 one-chord swaps across the three genres (IV ↔ iv, v ↔ V, …). Euro has almost none.
- **Synthpop varies by luck.** True Faith's and The Different Story's choruses differ by one note,
  so swapping between them produces new combinations; it has more generic in-between entries too.

The analysis docs already explain _why_ each record's harmony works ([analysis.md](../analysis.md)
§3 "Shared DNA", §7; [italo/](../italo/); [references/](../references/)). The generator just
doesn't use that knowledge: it stores the result, not the reasons.

## 2. Compatibility: classic progressions

- **Saved songs are already safe.** "My songs" and `#song=` links store a creation's full sections
  and progressions (spec.ts), so no change to the generator moves them. The snapshot test's legacy
  links keep checking that.
- **`ComposeOptions.chords: "classic" | "theory"`.** `"classic"` runs today's `prog()` in
  composer.ts and nothing else: same RNG streams, same draws. The snapshot test pins every existing
  case to `"classic"` and must not change.
- **The setting.** A **Progressions** select in the controls (theory / classic), stored in prefs.
  New installs and existing prefs default to **theory**.
- **Seed links.** `#seed=N` without a parameter plays **classic**, so every link shared so far
  keeps its song. New links write `#seed=N&chords=theory`. ♥ Save and ⤴ Share already store the full
  song, so they don't need it.
- **Everything else follows the setting**: next songs, DJ mode, the dice. Originals play their
  measured chords as before.
- **The wizard** keeps its measured presets, and gains "generate" entries that call the theory
  generator for the chosen style point.

The theory generator draws from its own forked streams (`root.fork("harmony:…")`), so tempo, key,
kit and the planners' draws are the same whichever mode is on. Only the chords differ.

## 3. The chord vocabulary

`theory.ts` maps a fixed list of numeral strings (`"VII/i"`, `"IV7sus4"`, …) to chords. A generator
needs to build chords that aren't in the list.

- Make `chord()` **compositional**: a degree numeral (case = quality, with ♭ implied by the minor
  scale as today), an optional quality suffix (`7`, `maj7`, `add9`, `sus4`, `7sus4`, `9sus4`, `ø7`),
  an optional applied target (`V/vi`), an optional slash bass (a degree, or `/3`).
- Every existing numeral must parse to the same `{ root, quality, bass }` as now (a test over the
  whole old table), so classic songs, creations and links don't move.
- `scaleForChord` already derives borrowed tones from a chord's notes, so the melodies follow any
  chord the parser accepts. Add tests for the new combinations the generator emits.

## 4. What the generator knows: the harmonic analysis as data

Each reference gets an **analysis** (new `src/harmony/`), written from the docs, beside its bank
entries, which stay as the record of what was measured. An analysis says _why_ its progressions
work, as traits with weights. Like the other traits, they blend over the references' weights
(`Blend` with `by: { [ref]: … }`).

### 4.1 Traits

- **Palette and colour:** the chords of the mode and the borrowed ones it leans on, each with a
  weight.
  - TF: Aeolian plus the Dorian major IV.
  - DS: Aeolian, the harmonic-minor V at a line's end, and Dorian ii–IV in the turnaround.
  - YC: major, tonic held back, borrowed v, deceptive V/vi → IV.
  - The Night: relative major and ♭II.
  - Faces: the parallel major in the chorus, maj7 and sus colours.
  - Euro: Dorian IV⁷ (HCW), Mixolydian ♭VII (CJ), half-diminished vi (IL).
- **Motion:** how the root or bass moves bar to bar, as weighted root-motion classes rather than
  chord pairs.
  - The ♭VI–♭VII–i stepwise climb.
  - The swing into the relative major: i → ♭III → ♭VII.
  - A descent by step: the royal road ♭VI–♭VII–v–i.
  - Falling fifths: HCW's upper chords over the IV pedal.
  - A two-chord vamp: TN i–♭VII, SC i–v, YC IV–V.
- **Bass treatment:** root position, a pedal under moving chords (IL on v, HCW on IV, TN's F under
  ♭VII), and passing inversions (GC's IV⁶ walk, YC's I⁶ on bar 4).
- **Where the tonic sits:**
  - On bar 1 (TF, DS).
  - Held back until the loop's end (Faces).
  - Never on a downbeat (YC's loop).
  - Only over another bass (IL's I/v).
- **Cadence (the loop's last bar):**
  - Home by step or plagal (♭VII → i, iv → i).
  - The Dorian lift (IV → i).
  - Ending on the dominant (GC's IV/V → V, SC's v, Faces' V7sus4).
  - Deceptive (YC's V/vi → IV).
  - Open, where the loop runs on without landing.
- **Rhythm:**
  - Loop length (2, 4 or 8 bars).
  - Chords per bar and where bars split (TF's VI–VII in bar 2, Faces' two chords to the bar).
  - Sus → resolved pairs inside a bar (VII7sus4–VII7, IVsus4–IV).
- **Second line:** whether an 8-bar progression repeats its 4 or answers it.
  - TF's MIDI verse's second line moves differently.
  - DS turns its second line toward V.

### 4.2 Section relations

Also traits, since they're where songs differ most:

- **Chorus vs verse:**
  - Shift to the relative major (The Night).
  - Same opening, new cadence (TF/DS: verse v, chorus IV/iv).
  - The same loop with louder parts (SC, YC).
  - Faster chord changes after a pedal verse (IL, HCW).
  - The parallel major (Faces).
- **Pre-chorus:** builds to the chorus's first chord through its dominant or a ♭VI–V turn (YC's
  V7/vi, IL's ♭VI–V, CJ's IV⁷sus4 stop).
- **Interlude, turnaround, bridge, lift:**
  - A turnaround leads back into the verse's first chord.
  - A bridge visits the subdominant side (TF's i–IV vamp, DS's ii–IV–V, IL's Aeolian iv).
  - A lift is the major key a fourth up (TF's outro).

These come from the docs and the bank, written by hand with a source line each, not fitted
automatically. The bank entries become the tests: §6 checks that each reference's analysis, at that
reference's weight alone, can produce its own measured loops.

## 5. The generator

Per song, on its own streams:

1. **Read the traits** at the song part's blend (merged across genres the way `pickFromBanks`
   mixes them today).
2. **Write the chorus first:** it's the hook.
   - **Choose a schema:** a slot pattern from the motion and tonic traits, e.g. `T – climb – climb
– cadence`, `vamp A – vamp B`, `pedal(X): moving chords`, `descent to T`.
   - **Fill the slots** from the palette, weighted by root motion. The cadence slot comes from the
     cadence traits.
   - **Add rhythm and colour:** bar splits, sus pairs, 7ths and add9s, and the bass treatment.
3. **Derive the verse** from the chorus through a section relation, then the pre-chorus (if the
   form has one), interlude, turnaround, bridge and lift from theirs. A section's progression is
   built against its neighbours: a turnaround resolves into the verse's first chord, and a
   pre-chorus into the chorus's.
4. **Score and choose.**
   - Generate several candidates per section and score them:
     - smooth bass motion (steps and fourths over leaps);
     - no chord repeated across a bar line unless it's a pedal or a vamp;
     - the cadence actually prepares the loop's restart;
     - the colour chord used once per line, not everywhere;
     - the contrast with the verse is audible.
   - Pick by weighted chance among the best, so near-ties still vary.
   - A candidate identical to a bank entry loses points, unless it's a quote.
5. **Quotes:** about 1 song in 10 plays one section (usually the chorus) on the measured loop of
   its strongest reference, as a nod to the record.

Everything stays deterministic from the seed. The output is a `Progressions` object as today, so
the planners, arranger, melodies and creator need no change.

## 6. Checking it

- **Snapshot:** classic songs unchanged (the existing snapshot). Theory songs get their own
  snapshot cases once the sound is accepted.
- **Theory tests:**
  - Every generated chord parses.
  - Each loop's cadence matches its trait.
  - A pre-chorus resolves into its chorus, and a turnaround into its verse.
  - At each reference's weight alone, the generator reaches that reference's measured loops among
    its candidates (the analysis is complete enough to explain them).
- **Variety metrics** (the table in §1, as a script in `scripts/`). Targets, without quotes:
  - under 10% of verses and choruses exactly a bank entry;
  - at least 3× today's distinct verse and chorus counts per genre;
  - at least 4 distinct progressions per song in every genre.
- **By ear.** Render offline (silent) and compare classic vs theory, the same seed, for 3 songs per
  genre plus 2 cross-genre blends, as clips named by file and section. Each style corner gets a
  check that it still sounds like its references. A theory song that sounds generic or wrong sends
  its trait or score back for a fix, not the song to the classic generator.

## 7. Phases

1. **Plumbing.**
   - ✅ The `chords` option, pref, select, `#seed=…&chords=` link parameter, and the classic default
     for old links. `Song.chords` records the mode; links are written and read by `links.ts`.
     Differences from §2: changing the select recomposes the playing generated song in place, as
     the kit and genre do (other changes, a genre or kit, keep a linked song's own mode). The
     "My songs" lookup key gains `/theory` for theory songs, so a saved classic song isn't taken
     for its theory twin once they differ.
   - ✅ At first theory mode just runs classic, so nothing audible changes (`// Phase 2:` in
     composer.ts marks where the generator plugs in).
   - ✅ The compositional `chord()` with its round-trip test over the old table
     (`src/chords.test.ts`). The grammar is in the comment above `parseNumeral` in theory.ts. A
     slash after an unaltered uppercase V (any quality) names an applied target (`V7/vi`), except
     over the tonic (`V/i` is V over a tonic pedal); after any other chord it names the bass. An
     explicit accidental (`b`, `#`, `♮`) counts from the major scale, for chords the implied
     minor-key degrees can't name (`♮VI`, `bvi`).
2. **Synthpop analysis and generator,** verse and chorus (and the major-key pre-chorus). It's the
   best-documented genre and the closest to the current results, so it's the easiest place to tell
   whether "theory" beats "classic" by ear.
3. **Secondary sections**: interlude, turnaround, bridge and lift, with their relations.
4. **Italo and Euro analyses:** pedals and slash basses, ♭II, the parallel-major chorus, sus pairs,
   2-bar loops, 10- and 14-bar choruses. Then cross-genre blends through the merged planner.
5. **Wizard "generate" entries,** variety script in `scripts/`, README and analysis updates.
6. **Theory becomes the default** once the ear checks pass for every genre.

## 8. Later: forms

The same idea for forms: the reasons behind each record's form, rather than one record's opening
or second half picked whole. Under the same switch (classic forms stay as they are):

- the energy curve (where layers enter and drop out);
- verse/chorus contrast;
- staggered instrument entries;
- where instrumental sections fall and which chords they play (today Italo's breaks reuse the
  verse loop, hence its 3.1 progressions per song);
- section length from phrase structure (a 4-bar loop played twice) rather than from the measured
  progression's length (IL's 14-bar chorus, CJ's 10).

That changes the planners, including the single-genre ones in `legacy-planners/`. Their
"change nothing" rule then holds for classic mode only. It gets its own plan once the chords are in.
