# Cross-genre blending

Issue #3. Today a genre is a wall: a song blends only between its own genre's references, with
its genre's planner, bank and traits. The goal is a freeform blend across all ten references:

- synthpop: True Faith, The Different Story, You Came
- Italo: The Night, Get Closer, Faces, Self Control
- Euro-disco: Innocent Love, Heaven Can Wait, Crazy Juliet

The genre becomes a preset, not a boundary.

## Decisions (2026-11-12)

- **A blend per part, not one per song.** Each part group has its own blend, so True Faith's
  bass can play under Get Closer's echo hook. A **simple mode** drives every part from one blend,
  which is the current behaviour.
- **Discrete traits come from the part's own blend.** Inside a part, a discrete trait (the
  riff's kind, a patch, the drum pattern) still comes from the reference that outweighs the
  others in that part's blend. The user controls each part, so a deterministic pick is easier to
  steer than a weighted draw.
- **A merged planner from the start.** We don't fall back to one genre's planner. A single
  planner draws every reference's blocks, with odds from the form blend.
- **Open mode: pick 2–n references, then a polygon.** Genre presets keep their polygons. "Open"
  lets the user tick any 2–n references, and the pad becomes a polygon of those.
- **Tempo: warn, don't limit.** Any references can mix. The UI notes when the chosen references'
  tempo centres are more than about 10 BPM apart.
- **Backward compatibility is exact.** Old links, saved songs and settings map onto the new
  model, and `snapshot.test.ts` keeps every existing song byte-identical.
- **The lead synth belongs to Riff.** The Night's and Crazy Juliet's lead-synth sections are
  placed by the form, and their sound and line come from the Riff part's blend.
- **Tempo follows the Song part**, with form, chords and key. Drums from a 123 BPM record can
  play at 110.
- **Auto sometimes draws a cross-genre blend.** Mostly it picks a random preset as today. About
  1 song in 5 gets a random open blend of 2–3 references from at least two genres, still in simple
  mode.
- **The legacy planners stay, in their own folder.** `planSections`, `planItalo` and `planEuro`
  move to `src/legacy-planners/` and keep serving single-genre form blends, so those songs never
  change. The merged planner serves blends that span genres.

## How the current model works (what changes)

- `Genre` (`src/genres.ts`) owns everything: `references`, the bank, substitutions, kits, bass
  figures, tempo and keys, its planner's odds (`form`, `italo`, `euro`), `drums`/`drumTraits`,
  `voices`/`voiceTraits`, `parts`, `bassTone`, `leadTone`, `mix` and `leadRanges`.
- A song's weights are an array in its genre's `references` order. `genreAt(genre, weights)`
  evaluates each `Blend` as `base + Σ by[ref] × w(ref)`. The `base` is a genre-level prior that
  belongs to no reference.
- `compose()` (`src/composer.ts`) picks one genre and dispatches to `planSections`, `planItalo`
  or `planEuro`.
- Links and saved songs store `g` (genre) and `w` (that genre's weights), or the legacy scalar
  `st`.

## The core idea: a blend as a mixture of genres

A `Blend`'s `base` makes it impossible to move references between genres without changing
numbers, so the genres' profiles stay as they are. A cross-genre value is a **mixture** of
per-genre values:

```
W         global weights, one per reference (all ten)
s_g       Σ W(r) for r in genre g             (the genre's share)
value     Σ_g (s_g / Σ s) × blend_g(W restricted to g, normalised by s_g)
```

When one genre holds all the weight, the mixture is that genre's blend **evaluated on the raw,
unnormalised weights**: literally today's call, so the result is bit-for-bit the same. Every
existing song has a single-genre blend, so the snapshot holds by construction. Only a blend that
spans genres runs the mixed path.

The same idea covers the parts that aren't plain numbers:

| What                                                                    | Mixed across genres                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Blend` values (tempo centre, bass tone, voice mix, odds, fader levels) | the mixture above                                                                                                          |
| Weighted choices (kits, bass figures, key tonics, major/minor)          | one combined list, each genre's entries × its share, in genre order                                                        |
| Progression bank                                                        | the union of the genres' pools, each entry's weight × its genre's share; style-axis entries read the genre's own `styleOf` |
| Substitutions                                                           | concatenated in genre order, each chance × its genre's share                                                               |
| Tempo range                                                             | the union of the genres' ranges in play; the centre and floor are mixtures                                                 |
| Lead ranges, titles                                                     | from the genre that outweighs the others in the relevant blend                                                             |
| Discrete traits (drums, voices, part styles)                            | the part blend's dominant reference; on a tie, the dominant genre's defaults                                               |

With one genre at share 1, each list is that genre's list with its weights × 1, so the RNG draws
the same.

## Parts

Each part group gets its own blend over the global references:

| Part      | Drives                                                                                           |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Song**  | form (the merged planner's odds), chords (bank, substitutions), key, tempo, ending, title        |
| **Drums** | kit, `DrumTraits` (pattern, hats, fills, grooves, kick weight), the drums fader                  |
| **Bass**  | bass figure, `BassTone`, the bass fader                                                          |
| **Riff**  | riff kind and patch, the hook, the lead synth (patch and line), the riff/hook/synth faders       |
| **Keys**  | pads, stabs, arp, sequencer, guitar: their patches, `parts.stabs`, `padTone`, `arp` kind, faders |
| **Voice** | `LeadTone`, lead ranges, countervoice, the counter-line odds, stacked voices, voice faders       |

Each `Track` (`instruments.ts`) maps to exactly one part, so `mixAt` reads each fader's level
from its own part's blend.

**Simple mode** sets all six to the same blend. The song then stores one blend, and links stay
short.

**Couplings to handle.** Some traits span parts today:

- Self Control's riff plays in unison with its bass (riff "unison", bass figure "sc").
- The Night's and Crazy Juliet's lead-synth sections are form blocks played on a riff-part patch.
- Faces' verse swaps pads for gated stabs (`verseWorld`): a form decision that depends on the keys
  part.

The rule: the form decides **where** a part plays, and the part's blend decides **what** plays.
Where a block needs a specific part (a "unison" riff with no matching bass figure), it falls back
to the part's own figure. Each coupling gets a test.

## The merged planner

All three planners share one skeleton:

```
opening → verse (+ pre-chorus) → chorus → after-chorus block → verse 2 (+ pre) → chorus
        → middle block → [verse 3 → chorus] → late block → last chorus(es) → ending
```

The merged planner walks that skeleton. At each slot it draws one block from the references that
offer one, in proportion to the form blend's weights. Each slot draws on its own forked stream.
Every block is tagged with its reference:

| Slot                   | Blocks (reference)                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opening                | drums-and-bass intro then riff intro (TF, DS, YC); tonic pedal (TN); build (FC); instrumental hook (GC); pad then unison riff (SC); staggered entries (CJ); solo motif then groove (HCW); pads over 16th hats, climb, link (IL) |
| Pre-chorus             | You Came's, entered from a pushed tonic (YC); title line (SC); Euro pre-chorus, maybe ending on a stop bar (IL, CJ, HCW); none (TF, DS, TN, GC, FC)                                                                             |
| After the first chorus | TF instrumental and VI–VII turn; DS interlude, turnaround and break; straight on (YC, CJ); 8-bar instrumental (FC, GC); stab break (SC); tag on i (HCW); stop on ♭VII (IL)                                                      |
| Middle                 | TF turnaround, bass solo and instrumental chorus; DS block; YC breakdown and bridge; TN reprise; CJ lead choruses; HCW instrumental chorus and break on V; IL instrumental link, verse and pre-chorus                           |
| Late                   | YC 16th-hat break and instrumental; FC/GC breakdown and lead-synth instrumental; IL bridge                                                                                                                                      |
| Last choruses          | double chorus (synthpop); last chorus (Italo, Euro); SC i–v vamp outro                                                                                                                                                          |
| Ending                 | lift to IV (TF); chorus to fade (all); IV held (YC); vamp (SC)                                                                                                                                                                  |

**Song-wide conventions** (block length 8 or 16, verse length, chorus length, whether the kick
ever stops) are drawn once per song from the form blend, through the genre that owns each
convention:

- synthpop's 8-bar choruses
- Italo's 16-bar blocks
- Euro's chorus that lasts as long as its progression

The chorus length follows the chorus progression's own source: each bank entry already knows
its reference or genre.

**Section dressing** is a song-wide flag drawn once from the form blend and tagged by reference.
Examples:

- Italo choruses keep the arp out.
- Heaven Can Wait's verses are bare.
- You Came stacks its chorus voices.

**Byte-identity.** A single-genre form blend keeps calling its genre's legacy planner unchanged.
The merged planner runs only when the form blend spans genres. The legacy planners are kept for
good, but apart:

- `src/legacy-planners/` holds one file per planner: `synthpop.ts` (`planSections`), `italo.ts`
  (`planItalo`) and `euro.ts` (`planEuro`).
- The pieces they share with the merged planner move to a common form module: `LAYERS_OFF`,
  `PlannedSection`, `placeSections` and `intoTonic`.
- The merged planner can reuse the legacy planners' block bodies only by copying them into
  tagged blocks, never by calling into the folder. That way an edit to a merged block can't move
  a single-genre song.
- The move itself is a pure code move, made first in phase 2 and checked against the snapshot.

## Data model

- `REFERENCE_IDS`: one global, ordered list of the ten references (genre order, then each genre's
  `references` order).
- `RefWeights`: a sparse `Partial<Record<ReferenceId, number>>`, so adding an eleventh reference
  never shifts anything.
- `PartBlends`: `Record<Part, RefWeights>`. Simple mode is all six equal.
- `Song` keeps `genre` and `weights` for display, `songOrigin` and old paths, as the song part's
  dominant genre and that genre's array. It gains `blends: PartBlends`. `GenreAt` gives way to a
  `ProfileAt` per part, with the same `at(blend)`, `drums` and `voices` API, so the arranger and
  instruments change little.
- Genre profiles keep their data. Moving each reference's measured data into a `references.ts`
  record (the issue's step 1) is a later tidy-up: the mixture makes it unnecessary for blending.

**Links and storage.** Old `g` + `w`/`st` decode to a single-genre blend for every part, with
the exact old weights. New links write (as built in phase 3, inside the creation's JSON):

- `b`: the song part's blend, by reference id, e.g. `{"true-faith":0.5,"get-closer":0.5}`
- `bp`: only the parts that differ from it

A single-genre simple-mode song still writes the old `g`/`w` form, so its links don't change.
`legacy-links.json` keeps decoding.

## UI

- **Genre select**: the three presets, "Open", and "Auto". A preset shows its polygon as today.
  "Open" shows a reference picker (ten chips grouped by genre). The pad becomes a polygon of the
  ticked references (2–n), in picker order.
- **Simple / per part**: a toggle under the pad. Per part shows six rows: Song, Drums, Bass, Riff,
  Keys and Voice. Each row is "same as the main pad" or its own preset/Open choice with a small
  pad.
- **Tempo warning**: in the existing tempo note, when the song blend's references span more than
  about 10 BPM of tempo centres.
- **Creator** (`creator.ts`): the same controls, stored on the creation's spec.
- **Auto**: a random preset and a random point per song, as today. About 1 song in 5 instead
  gets a random open blend of 2–3 references from at least two genres, in simple mode. That chance
  draws on its own forked stream, so the preset songs draw as before. Auto seeds that land on an
  open blend do play differently from today. That touches nothing stored: Auto-genre songs aren't
  in the snapshot, and saved songs and links record the concrete blend.
