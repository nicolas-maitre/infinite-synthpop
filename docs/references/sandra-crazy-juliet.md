## 8. Written sources

- **Release:** track 6 of Sandra's third album, **_Into a Secret Land_**, released on 24 October
  1988 by Virgin, 4:11. It was not a single; the album's singles were "Heaven Can Wait", "Secret
  Land", "We'll Be Together" and "Around My Heart".
- **Writers:** lyrics by Klaus Hirschburger; music by Hubert Kemmler, Michael Cretu and Markus Löhr
  (Wikipedia's track listing; a lyrics site gives the same four names).
- **Production:** produced and mixed by Michael Cretu, with Frank Peterson also mixing. Recorded at
  Sandra and Cretu's home studio in Ibiza, where they had moved and married in January 1988 (the
  infobox calls it A.R.T.). Cretu's earlier work was done at his Data Alpha studio in Munich, on
  which A.R.T. was modelled.
- **Personnel:** Sandra lead vocals; Hubert Kemmler backing vocals (uncredited). It is the only
  Sandra album on which Kemmler co-wrote every song, and her last with his signature backing
  vocals until _Stay in Touch_ (2012). No source names the players, the programmers, the drum
  machine or the synths.
- **Charts (album):** Switzerland 9, Finland 10, Austria 13, Germany 14, France 14, Norway 18,
  Sweden 22; platinum in France and Switzerland.
- **Versions:** no single, maxi or extended version of "Crazy Juliet" was found. For a 12″ mode
  (issue #2), the record's own part-muted moments are the intro's staggered entries (pad and
  sequence; then bass; hats; the full kit without bass) and the two instrumental choruses.

**Harmonically, Crazy Juliet is a synthpop song, and it touches all three references.** Its
chorus, heard locally, is the VI–VII climb of TF and DS, landing on a minor root. Its verse/chorus
contrast is the one-note Dorian/Aeolian switch that separates TF's chorus from DS's. And like You
Came, its chorus loops without ever sounding the tonic. DS is the nearest neighbour: it was made
with the same producer (Michael Cretu), and its verse rests on the same minor v.

**Rhythmically, it belongs to neither group.** Every synthpop and Italo reference measured so far
has a kick on every beat and a bass on the 8ths. Crazy Juliet has a backbeat pop kick (1 and 3
with syncopated ghosts), a syncopated bass that rests and glides, and the fastest tempo of all.
Those are the traits that make a generated song sound like this record, and dropping them into
the synthpop genre would change what a synthpop corner means.

**Verdict: a separate "Euro-disco" genre**, with Crazy Juliet as a reference, if the other Sandra
songs share the kick and bass habits; the sessions measuring "Heaven Can Wait" and "Innocent Love"
will tell. The label fits loosely: the kick isn't four-on-the-floor, so the genre would be a
Cretu/Sandra Euro-pop sound more than disco. If the other Sandra songs turn out four-on-the-floor
with 8th-note bass, Crazy Juliet still sits apart from them on rhythm. It could then join synthpop
next to DS only if the generator gets the drum and bass traits below as per-reference traits.

**New building blocks it would need:**

1. **Drum traits:**
   - a backbeat kick (1 and 3, never 2 or 4) with a 4-bar cycle of syncopated ghost kicks
     (2a │ 2&+4& │ 2a │ 3&+4&), 4–7 dB under;
   - 16th hats that skip the "e" after each backbeat;
   - a crash on every section and phrase downbeat, with the hats skipping 1e–1a;
   - a snare run (2, 2e, 3, 3e, 4, 4&, 4a) in each chorus's last bar, and a 4e–4& pickup at the
     end of each 8-bar phrase;
   - a stop bar before the chorus;
   - a quieter snare on 1 in some chorus bars.
2. **Bass styles:**
   - a held root with syncopated re-strikes (1, &, 2&, 2a, 3&), a lower-neighbour flick on 3e and
     a pickup on 4& (the ♭7 below, or a chromatic or rising step into the next root);
   - a legato chorus line through the chord roots on 1 – 2& (glide on 3) – 4& │ 1 – 3& – 4&,
     dropped an octave from the verse, octave-layered;
   - register D♯1–F♯2, dark (harmonics up to 300–420 Hz).
3. **Progressions** (in a major key with minor-side borrowing):
   - verse I I I I │ ii │ ♭VII │ I I (Mixolydian);
   - pre-chorus iv │ ♭III(add9) │ ♭VII │ IV⁷sus4 │ IV⁷ (4 + a stop bar), resolving IV⁷ → ♭III;
   - chorus (♭III IV │ v) ×3 │ ♭III │ ii⁷ │ V sus4 │ V¹¹, the V¹¹ going to I (into a verse) or back
     to ♭III (into another chorus). The chorus never sounds the tonic.
   - Substitutions it demonstrates: the 3rd of the key switching between sections (E♯ ↔ E), iv →
     IV⁷ on the same root, sus4 → 7sus4 on a held chord, IV⁷ → ♭III, and a suspended dominant
     that never resolves to its major triad.
4. **Form blocks:** a 5-bar pre-chorus (4 + stop), a 10-bar chorus (6 + 4-bar tag), back-to-back
   choruses with two instrumental ones in the middle, no bridge and no breakdown, a fade on the
   chorus; an intro built from the verse progression with staggered entries.
5. **A riff register and a lead role:** a syncopated high riff around G♯5–F♯5–D♯5 in the choruses,
   and a lead line that carries whole instrumental choruses. The generator writes its own; the
   original's notes are the composition.
6. **A low, voice-like synth answer** at the end of each 8-bar verse phrase, an octave under the
   lead. The sound is specific to this record; the role (a one-bar answer at phrase ends) is what
   the generator would take.
7. **Tempo** around 123, above the synthpop range (116–121).
