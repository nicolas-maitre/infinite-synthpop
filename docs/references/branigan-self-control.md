## 3. Harmony

### 3.1 Tonal centre: F♯ minor

The song is in **F♯ minor (Aeolian)**, not C♯ minor as the key profile says.

- **Every chord belongs to F♯ natural minor:** F♯m, C♯m, E, Bm, D, A. C♯ minor would need D♯ and
  B♯. Instead, D natural is a chord tone in two chords (Bm and D). Of the modes on C♯, only
  Phrygian fits the notes, and nothing in the record leans on C♯ as a resting point.
- **The 8-bar loop starts on F♯m and returns to it.** Every phrase opens on F♯m, and the loop's
  last chord (A, III) falls back to F♯m at every join.
- **The record begins on F♯m7 and fades on the i–v vamp.** The intro pad was confirmed as F♯m7
  by ear.
- **Why the profile says C♯ minor:** C♯ is the loudest pitch class (it is in i, v and III, and the
  bass sits on it a quarter of the time). Ghost notes also inflate it (below). Hooktheory, Karaoke
  Version and AcousticBrainz all give F♯ minor.

**Ghost thirds.** In the pad register, the chroma shows A♯ over F♯ and E♯ over C♯, as if the
chords were major. By ear (§10, checks 1–3) every one of those chords is minor. A♯ is the **5th
harmonic of F♯**, and E♯ the 5th harmonic of C♯: bright pads and a low sawtooth bass put them
there. The same caution applies to every major-third reading in this song. The minor thirds (A
over F♯, E over C♯, D over B) can't be harmonics, and the voice confirms them.

- **Tempo:** 106.69 BPM from a straight line through 421 beats. Per 32 beats it holds within
  106.56–106.78, and the residual drift is ±18 ms over the whole song: a machine clock, with the
  slow drift of tape or a transfer.
- **Tuning:** the single and the instrumental sit about 20 cents flat (A4 ≈ 435 Hz). The bass
  reads flatter, at −37 to −46 cents, probably a pitch-estimation effect of its weak fundamental.
  The 12″ sits about 5 cents sharp.

## 9. Placement

### 9.1 Against the synthpop references

| Axis         | True Faith (TF)                    | The Different Story (DS)          | You Came (YC)                       | **Self Control**                                                      |

| Bass         | 8ths, next root on 4a, G1–F2       | 8ths, next root on 4a, A1–B2      | root 8ths, same root on 4a, G♯1–F♯2 | **staccato quarters on the root**, no anticipation, **C♯1–B1**        |

The loop shares DNA with the synthpop references. Its first half, i–v, is TF's verse colour (the
melancholy minor v). Its VII–iv–VI–III descent uses the same Aeolian chords as DS's chorus. And,
like You Came, it carries the whole song on one loop. But the groove is different in every
measured respect: 10–13 BPM slower, a quarter-note bass instead of 8ths, no running hats in the
verse, claps in the chorus. Played at 117–120 BPM with an 8th bass, it would be a different
record.

### 9.2 Against Italo disco and the Sandra songs

Sibling analyses are in progress, so this compares against their hand-over notes.

- **Italo** (Valerie Dore's "The Night", 110.3 BPM; Clio's "Faces", 120.1): a kick on every beat,
  claps or snare on 2 and 4, loops shared between sections, root bass (8ths in "The Night"), few
  fills. Self Control matches the kick, the backbeat, the shared loop, the minor-key loop
  harmony and the scarcity of fills. Its 106.7 BPM is just under "The Night".
- **Sandra** ("Crazy Juliet", "Heaven Can Wait", "Innocent Love", 115–123 BPM): the kick is not
  four-on-the-floor (1 and 3, or backbeat patterns with syncopations), and the bass is syncopated.
  Those are the traits that would define a Euro-disco genre, and Self Control has neither.

### 9.3 Verdict: Italo disco

**Self Control belongs with Italo disco**, as its most polished, slowest corner. It doesn't
justify a separate Euro-disco genre, and it isn't synthpop.

- The composition is Italian: Raf's Italo hit, arranged by Celso Valli. Branigan's version keeps
  its form logic: one minor loop for everything, a four-on-the-floor kick, a bass on the beat.
- What the Munich/LA production changed is the surface. The keyboard hook became a guitar riff,
  and the vocal break gained the stab on 2. There are session-style claps, a big lead voice, and a
  clean mix. None of that touches the genre's rhythmic skeleton.
- It is **the bridge the user expected**, but toward a polished Euro-pop sound, not toward
  Sandra. Sandra's songs differ from Italo in the kick and the bass, and Self Control keeps
  Italo's kick and bass. If the Sandra songs form a "Euro-disco" genre, Self Control stays on the
  Italo side of the line.
- **Caveat:** "Maria Magdalena", which the user hears as similar, wasn't measured here. If it has
  Self Control's four-on-the-floor and quarter-note bass, the similarity is Italo-style groove in
  a Sandra record, and worth checking before the Euro-disco genre is fixed.

### 9.4 New building blocks it would need

1. **Tempo:** extend the Italo range down to about **106 BPM**.
2. **An 8-bar Aeolian loop for every section:** i–v–i–v–VII–iv–VI–III. The minor v throughout;
   no V, no Dorian IV. In the relative major it is vi–iii–vi–iii–V–ii–IV–I, a different shape from
   the synthpop bank's.
3. **Form by arrangement over a fixed loop:**
   - an intro of 4 bars of pad on i7 with percussion, then 4 bars of riff with a syncopated bass
     on i–v;
   - verse 16 (no running hats);
   - pre-chorus 16 (16th hats; it carries the hook), which can be cut or halved later;
   - chorus 16 (claps and a high part);
   - a **4-bar stab break**: one reverbed hit on beat 2 per bar, voice only, over i–v;
   - 2 bars of drums and voice;
   - a half-length verse 2;
   - an **i–v vamp outro** to the fade.
4. **A 12″ mode** (issue #2), on this song's model: double the intro and the drums-only bars,
   restore a half-length second pre-chorus, and add 12 bars to the outro vamp. Every added bar
   reuses an existing texture.
5. **A bass style:** **quarter notes on the root, an 8th long**, very low (C♯1–B1), a dark,
   low-passed saw with the 2nd harmonic loudest. Octave notes on some "&"s in the last choruses. A
   syncopated riff-unison figure for the intro (root on 1, 1a, 2&, then the third or fifth).
6. **Drum traits:**
   - no hats in the verse; 16ths from the pre-chorus;
   - in the chorus, a **clap layered on beat 4** and, every other bar, a syncopated clap on 3e and
     an open hat on 4a; in the outro the clap moves to beat 2;
   - a very short kick (−30 dB at 80 ms) and an ungated snare tail;
   - almost no fills.
7. **Arrangement roles:** offbeat chord chops on every "&" in verses; a syncopated riff (guitar on
   the record) in the intro; a bright high part in the choruses; a low reverbed stab for breaks.
