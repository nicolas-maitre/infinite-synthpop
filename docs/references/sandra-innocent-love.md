**Verdict: neither synthpop nor Italo. It belongs in a separate "Euro-disco" genre**, with the other
Cretu-produced Sandra songs if their measurements agree.

- **Against Italo:**
  - all three Italo references keep a kick on every beat and a root-8ths bass, and so does every Italo
    record they stand for;
  - Innocent Love's syncopated kick and melodic bass are the opposite habit;
  - its tempo is above The Night and Get Closer but below Faces, so tempo doesn't decide it.
- **Against synthpop:**
  - it shares some harmony with TF and DS: a minor key, a verse on the minor v, TF's chorus chords
    in another order, and DS's harmonic V into the chorus;
  - but the generator's synthpop engine is built on four-on-the-floor, a root-8ths bass with dense
    hats, and fills, and this song has none of those.
- **What a Euro-disco genre would be, on this evidence:**
  - a minor key that switches between Dorian, harmonic and Aeolian by section;
  - a circle-of-fifths chorus;
  - a backbeat kit with a syncopated kick and no running hats;
  - a melodic, syncopated, legato bass;
  - stops before each chorus;
  - a tempo around 115.
- **What would change the verdict:** if Crazy Juliet and Heaven Can Wait turn out to be
  four-on-the-floor with a root-8ths bass, Innocent Love is the odd one of Sandra's songs rather than
  the template. Then the genre would need a "mid-80s" corner, or this song would stay unassigned.
  Their automatic first passes (unverified; their sessions will settle them) read 123.0 BPM for Crazy
  Juliet and 118.7 for Heaven Can Wait.

## 10. New building blocks it would need

1. **Harmony:**
   - **Chorus loop:** i │ IV │ ♭VII │ ♭III, one chord per bar, with the IV major (Dorian) moving
     sus4 → major, and the ♭VII suspended (9sus4, no third).
   - **Verse:** a v pedal under v │ ii7/5 │ v │ I/5, with the ii minor and the I major (both Dorian),
     8 bars, twice.
   - **Pre-chorus:** i │ viø7 │ i │ viø7, the Dorian 6th.
   - **Turn into the chorus:** ♭VI │ V (major), the V with a stop.
   - **Bridge:** ♭VI │ ♭VI–♭VII │ i │ ♭III │ ♭VII │ iv │ ♭III │ i │ ♭VI │ ♭VI, all Aeolian.
   - The key machinery needs a **per-section mode**: which forms of the 6th and 7th a section uses.
2. **Form blocks:**
   - an intro alternating i and v, 8 bars without the bass, then 4 bars of ♭VI–♭VII with the voice;
   - a 2-bar drums-out link where the bass runs down into the verse;
   - a verse block of 8 + 4 + 2 bars (pedal, pre-chorus, turn with a stop);
   - a 16-bar chorus that ends in a 2-bar stop on ♭VII;
   - an instrumental block (a 4-bar riff link, then a verse with an instrumental lead);
   - a late 10-bar Aeolian bridge;
   - a chorus-to-fade ending.
   - For issue #2, the stops, the links, the bass-less intro and the instrumental block are ready-made
     sections where only some parts play.
3. **Bass styles:**
   - **verse:** a pedal on 1, 2a, 3, 3& with a pickup a step below on 4& (A♭–F every 4th bar);
   - **chorus:** struck on 1, 1&, 2e and 3&; slurred to the pentatonic neighbour below on 2&, back on
     3, and a two-note approach on 4 and 4& ending on the leading tone into i;
   - mono legato, a low register (D♭1–B♭1) with a weak fundamental and strong 2nd and 3rd partials.
4. **Drum traits:**
   - a backbeat kit with the kick on 1, 3 and 3& plus a ghost on 2a (verse), or 1, 2a, 3, 3& and 4&
     (chorus);
   - no running hats except 16ths in the intro and bridge, and an open hat on 1&;
   - stop bars (one hit on 2) instead of fills;
   - a short, pitch-dropping kick and a long, ungated, reverbed snare.
5. **Layers:** chord stabs in the chorus that follow the bass rhythm; a low verse voice and stacked
   chorus voices a fourth higher; a high synth line only in the instrumental link.
6. **Tempo:** about 115 BPM, below the synthpop range (116–121) the generator uses now.
